## One-Sentence Summary

Acetylcysteine is a thiol-based mucolytic and antioxidant that reduces disulfide bonds. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **thrombotic disease**, with **7 clinical trials** and **20 publications** compiled for this prediction. Most of the evidence concerns thrombotic microangiopathy and thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura (TTP), not thrombosis in general.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank is not available. Based on the data compiled for this report, acetylcysteine is a thiol-based reducing agent. It can break disulfide bonds in von Willebrand factor (VWF) multimers and reduce their size and platelet-binding activity. This is plausible for VWF-driven microvascular clotting such as TTP and transplant-associated thrombotic microangiopathy (TA-TMA).

It also has antioxidant activity, which may limit oxidative injury to blood vessel walls and platelet activation. Preclinical mouse and baboon TTP models support this mechanism (PMID 28011677).

The support is strongest for the thrombotic microangiopathy subset. Evidence for thrombosis in general (for example arterial or venous clots) is not shown in the compiled data. No cited study directly addresses "thrombotic disease" as a whole, so the evidence level is capped at L5 under the report rules, even though the related microangiopathy data are encouraging.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
No cited study directly addresses thrombotic disease as a whole, so the evidence level is L5. The trial and literature data are concentrated on TA-TMA and TTP, where a completed Phase 3 trial and a randomized prophylaxis study exist, but outcomes have not been confirmed in the compiled data.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Verified results of the completed Phase 3 trial (NCT03252925) and the randomized prophylaxis study (PMID 35940529)
- A decision on whether the target indication should be narrowed to thrombotic microangiopathy / TTP
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the Indian licences
- A safety review of the 247 recorded drug interactions for the target population
