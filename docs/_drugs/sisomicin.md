## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, sisomicin is an aminoglycoside antibiotic that inhibits bacterial protein synthesis by binding the 30S ribosomal subunit. Its use is in bacterial infections, not joint disease.

No mechanistic link to osteoarthritis has been established. Osteoarthritis is a chronic degenerative joint disease, and nothing in the available data suggests that a ribosome-targeting antibacterial would act on cartilage degeneration or joint inflammation. Aminoglycosides are also known for ototoxicity and nephrotoxicity, which makes them an unintuitive fit for long-term use in this condition.

The high score (98.20%) reflects proximity in the knowledge graph rather than biological evidence. The other top predictions point the same way: osteoarthritis susceptibility, rheumatoid arthritis, gout and several rare skeletal dysplasias. All are likely driven by graph proximity to osteoarthritis and carry no supporting evidence. Some of these rare skeletal conditions could theoretically be linked to aminoglycoside readthrough of nonsense mutations, but this is speculative and unsupported for sisomicin.

## Safety Considerations

Please refer to the package insert for safety information.

Aminoglycosides as a class are associated with ototoxicity and nephrotoxicity. These class-level risks would be a significant concern for any chronic use, and in gout, kidney effects could further disturb urate handling.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests on a knowledge-graph score alone. There are no clinical trials or publications, no established mechanism, and a class safety profile that is unfavorable for a chronic degenerative joint disease.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
