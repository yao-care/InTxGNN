# Omeprazole: From Gastric Ulcer and Acid-Related Disease to Duodenogastric Reflux

## One-Sentence Summary

Omeprazole is a proton pump inhibitor originally used for acid-related upper gastrointestinal conditions such as gastric ulcer, duodenal ulcer, gastroesophageal reflux disease and Zollinger-Ellison syndrome.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **duodenogastric reflux**, but the support is thin: **1 clinical trial** (a diagnostic imaging study that does not test omeprazole) and **15 publications**, mostly animal studies, small human studies and background articles.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, omeprazole is a proton pump inhibitor that suppresses gastric acid secretion. Its efficacy in acid-related conditions such as reflux disease, esophagitis and peptic ulcer is well established, and it may therefore relieve the acid-related mucosal injury and symptoms that accompany bile reflux.

The link is indirect. Omeprazole does not stop bile or duodenal contents from refluxing into the stomach. Small human studies in Barrett's oesophagus (PMIDs 10994616, 9824338) and a pediatric prospective study of oesophageal bile reflux (PMID 16641575) looked at its effect on bile reflux, and they are the main clinical-level evidence. Three rat studies (PMIDs 33027361, 10389684, 8943968) suggest that acid suppression combined with duodenogastric reflux may promote mucosal growth and gastric carcinogenesis. This is a safety signal that must be resolved before any repurposing.

A second predicted indication, duodenal obstruction (TxGNN score 99.64%), was also reviewed. It is a mechanical condition that acid suppression does not reverse. The high score probably reflects network proximity to duodenal ulcer rather than a true mechanistic link, and the evidence is limited to case reports and narrative articles. It is not recommended for further work (Hold).

## Safety Considerations

- **Knowledge-graph contraindication signals** (not label text): liver failure, liver disease, gallbladder disease, congestive heart failure, osteoporosis, postmenopausal osteoporosis, bone fracture and chronic atrophic gastritis.
- **Drug Interactions**: 673 interactions are recorded. Examples of **Major** interactions are acalabrutinib and atazanavir. Examples of **Moderate** interactions are alprazolam, atorvastatin, fenofibrate, hydrochlorothiazide, amphotericin B products and apalutamide.
- **Carcinogenesis concern**: rat studies suggest that acid suppression combined with duodenogastric reflux may promote gastric carcinogenesis.

Please refer to the package insert for warnings and contraindications; this label information was not available when the report was compiled.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The TxGNN score is high, but no trial tests omeprazole for duodenogastric reflux. The human evidence is limited to small studies in Barrett's oesophagus and children, and animal data raise a carcinogenesis concern. Omeprazole does not directly block bile reflux, so this is best treated as a research question rather than a repurposing candidate.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the Indian regulator, which are currently missing and block safety screening
- Detailed mechanism of action data (for example from DrugBank)
- A controlled clinical study of omeprazole specifically for duodenogastric reflux
- A long-term safety assessment of the gastric carcinogenesis signal in reflux settings
- Confirmation of approved indications for the Indian licences
