## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, dasatinib is a kinase inhibitor that acts on BCR-ABL, SRC-family kinases, c-KIT and PDGFR.

In Ewing sarcoma, preclinical studies show that SRC activity drives invadopodia formation, migration and invasion, especially under microenvironmental stress. Dasatinib inhibited migration, invasion and proliferation in sarcoma and Ewing sarcoma cell lines, and induced apoptosis in bone sarcoma cells that depend on SRC. The FAK-Src axis has been proposed as a therapeutic target in Ewing sarcoma and related tumours.

Clinical support is weaker. A review notes that single-agent dasatinib failed in the Ewing sarcoma and rhabdomyosarcoma subtypes of a Phase 2 advanced-sarcoma study. The remaining interest lies mainly in combination approaches, such as the RIST regimen (rapamycin, irinotecan, dasatinib, temozolomide), which has been used in relapsed disease.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
- The evidence level is L3. A Phase 2 sarcoma trial (NCT00464620) included a Ewing sarcoma cohort, and a small case series (PMID 42804591) used a dasatinib-containing regimen in relapsed disease. No randomized trial exists, and a review reports that single-agent dasatinib failed in the Ewing sarcoma subtype, so the signal is mostly preclinical and combination-based.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Ewing-specific outcomes from NCT00464620
- Prospective data on dasatinib-containing combinations such as RIST
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the Indian labels
- A safety monitoring plan, especially for cardiac, haematological and fluid-retention risks
