# Tobramycin: From Bacterial Eye and Meningeal Infections to Exposure Keratitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Tobramycin is an aminoglycoside antibiotic, originally used for bacterial infections including conjunctivitis, uveitis-related eye conditions, Staphylococcus aureus infection, and bacterial meningitis.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **exposure keratitis**, but there are **no clinical trials** and only **8 publications**, mostly case reports and laboratory studies that are indirect or even raise toxicity concerns.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on general knowledge, tobramycin is a broad-spectrum aminoglycoside that inhibits bacterial protein synthesis by binding the 30S ribosomal subunit. It is already used topically for bacterial eye infections.

Exposure keratitis is a corneal surface disorder caused by incomplete eyelid closure and drying. A damaged corneal surface is prone to secondary bacterial infection, so antibacterial coverage is plausible. The link is to infection prevention or treatment, not to the underlying disease.

There is also a counterpoint. Laboratory work shows aminoglycosides can be toxic to corneal epithelial cells, which is a concern when the epithelium is already compromised.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications (from the knowledge graph, not label text):** The drug-disease database lists conditions including kidney disease, myasthenia gravis and other neuromuscular junction disorders, Parkinson disease, herpes simplex virus keratitis, glaucoma, ocular hypertension, fungal and viral eye infections, ocular tuberculosis, and ophthalmic herpes zoster. Several of these are eye conditions and should be checked carefully against any corneal indication.
- **Drug Interactions:** 131 interactions were identified. Major interactions include **magnesium sulfate** and **mannitol**. Moderate interactions include amphotericin B, vancomycin, kanamycin, neomycin, aspirin, and several proton pump inhibitors (omeprazole, pantoprazole, lansoprazole, rabeprazole, dexlansoprazole). These are mainly relevant to systemic use.
- **Corneal toxicity:** Laboratory data show aminoglycosides can harm corneal epithelial cells, which matters for a disease with a damaged epithelium.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction score is very high, but there are no clinical trials and the literature is indirect (case reports of bacterial keratitis and laboratory studies). One laboratory study points to potential corneal epithelial toxicity. Tobramycin may help only with secondary infection, not with exposure keratitis itself.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications for the marketed eye products (currently missing, and a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Clinical data on tobramycin use in exposure keratitis, especially its effect on infection rates and corneal healing
- A safety assessment of corneal epithelial toxicity with topical use in a compromised cornea
- Review of the knowledge-graph contraindications against the eye conditions listed above
