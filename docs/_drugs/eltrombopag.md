## Eltrombopag: From Autoimmune Thrombocytopenia to HIV Infectious Disease
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Eltrombopag is a thrombopoietin receptor agonist, originally used to treat autoimmune thrombocytopenia (immune thrombocytopenia, ITP).
The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **HIV infectious disease**. In practice, the plausible benefit is for HIV-associated thrombocytopenia rather than for the infection itself.
Support currently comes from **1 clinical trial** (terminated, with an indirect link to HIV) and **8 publications**, mostly case reports and reviews.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Eltrombopag stimulates the thrombopoietin receptor to increase platelet production. Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the compiled data. Based on its known drug class, its efficacy in immune thrombocytopenia is established.

Thrombocytopenia is a common complication of HIV infection. It arises from immune-mediated platelet destruction, similar to ITP, and from impaired platelet production in the bone marrow. Stimulating the thrombopoietin receptor could therefore plausibly raise platelet counts in HIV-associated thrombocytopenia.

Case reports also describe benefit in HIV-related aplastic anaemia. One report noted immunomodulatory changes (reduced Th1 and Th17 cells, higher regulatory T-cell ratio). A separate in vitro screen reported modulation of HIV-1 proviral transcription, but this is preclinical and does not support antiviral claims. The high TxGNN score reflects this overlap but is a computational prediction, not clinical evidence.

## Safety Considerations

- **Knowledge-graph contraindication signals** (not label text): kidney disease, liver disease, acute liver failure, hepatitis, gallbladder disease, hyperbilirubinaemia (including Gilbert syndrome), anaemia, leukopenia, myelodysplastic syndrome, thrombocytosis, hepatic vein thrombosis and Budd-Chiari syndrome. These should be confirmed against the official package insert.
- **Drug Interactions**: 135 interactions were identified. The one **Major** interaction among those listed is **eluxadoline**. Moderate interactions include polyvalent cation products (calcium, magnesium and aluminium salts, iron, kaolin, attapulgite, magaldrate), which may reduce absorption, and glyburide.

Please refer to the package insert for the full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The mechanistic link to HIV-associated thrombocytopenia is plausible, and case-level reports are encouraging. However, there is no completed controlled trial in HIV, the only registered trial is terminated and indirectly related, and the benefit would be for the haematologic complication rather than HIV infection. The package insert safety data is also missing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Review of the termination reason and any results for NCT00678587, and confirmation of its relevance to HIV
- Controlled studies in HIV-associated thrombocytopenia
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the India licences, and the approved indication text
- Safety assessment in HIV populations, including liver function and interactions with antiretroviral therapy
