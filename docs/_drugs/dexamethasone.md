# Dexamethasone: From Adrenocortical Insufficiency and Inflammatory Conditions to Alopecia Areata

## One-Sentence Summary

Dexamethasone is a potent glucocorticoid, originally used for conditions such as adrenocortical insufficiency, Crohn disease, acute lymphoblastic leukaemia and other inflammatory or endocrine disorders.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **alopecia areata**,
with **no registered clinical trials** but **20 publications** retrieved, including one randomized controlled trial, one network meta-analysis and several cohort studies of oral mini-pulse dexamethasone.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not currently available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, dexamethasone is a potent glucocorticoid with strong anti-inflammatory and immunosuppressive effects. Its efficacy in immune-mediated and inflammatory conditions, such as inflammatory bowel disease and eosinophilic pneumonia, is well established.

Alopecia areata is an autoimmune, non-scarring hair loss disorder in which T cells attack the hair follicles after their immune privilege collapses (IFN-gamma and CD8+ NKG2D+ T-cell activity). A glucocorticoid can suppress this immune attack. Oral mini-pulse dosing (for example, 5 mg on two consecutive days each week) is used to limit cumulative steroid exposure while keeping the anti-inflammatory effect. Relapse after withdrawal is common, and the long-term safety of repeated pulses needs monitoring.

The other nine predicted indications are far less supported. Most have no trials or relevant literature, and for several (telogen effluvium, hereditary and genetic hair disorders, lacrimal gland prolapse, alopecia with antibody deficiency) a steroid mechanism is weak or potentially harmful. This report therefore focuses on alopecia areata.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications flagged in the knowledge graph (not label text):** hypertension, congestive heart failure, hyperglycaemia, epilepsy, herpes simplex virus keratitis, measles, certain urea cycle disorders (carbamoyl phosphate synthetase I deficiency, citrullinemia, hyperargininemia), and pituitary hormone deficiency conditions (including hypopituitarism and Sheehan syndrome).
- **Drug Interactions:** 913 interactions were recorded. Major ones in the sample reviewed include **fentanyl** and **adalimumab**. Moderate ones include paclitaxel, zidovudine, ibuprofen, ketorolac, nifedipine, acetazolamide, acarbose and abemaciclib.
- **Treatment-specific considerations:** Relapse after stopping is common. Repeated pulses need monitoring for cumulative steroid exposure. Immunosuppression may be harmful in patients with antibody deficiency or infection-driven conditions.

Package insert warnings and contraindications were not available, so please refer to the package insert for the full safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
For alopecia areata, the evidence includes one small randomized trial, a network meta-analysis and several cohort studies of dexamethasone mini-pulse therapy, and the drug is widely marketed in India. However, no registered clinical trials exist, the studies are mostly small and heterogeneous, and relapse after withdrawal is common. The other nine predicted indications should remain on **Hold**, as they rest on model prediction alone or have no relevant evidence.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from India Pharma (currently a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Detailed mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Larger, controlled trials of dexamethasone mini-pulse therapy against JAK inhibitors or other standard treatments
- A monitoring plan for cumulative steroid exposure, relapse and relevant comorbidities (hypertension, hyperglycaemia, infection risk)
- Approved indication text for the Indian licences, to confirm the labelled uses
