## Clotrimazole: From Superficial Fungal Infections to Acne
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Clotrimazole is an azole antifungal originally used for skin, mouth and vaginal fungal infections such as tinea pedis and candidiasis.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **acne**, but the supporting evidence is thin: **1 clinical trial** (suspended, a three-drug combination) and **no publications**.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data for clotrimazole was not available in the data compiled for this report. From general pharmacology, clotrimazole is an azole antifungal. It inhibits fungal CYP51 (lanosterol 14-alpha-demethylase), blocking ergosterol synthesis and damaging the fungal cell membrane. It also has reported antimicrobial and anti-inflammatory activity on the skin.

The plausible link to acne is indirect. Clotrimazole might reduce microbial burden (for example *Malassezia*) and local inflammation. Acne, however, is driven mainly by excess sebum, follicular hyperkeratinization and *Cutibacterium acnes*. Clotrimazole does not address these main drivers.

The very high TxGNN score is a computational prediction and does not replace clinical evidence. The only trial found tested clotrimazole inside a fixed combination, so it cannot show that clotrimazole itself helps acne.

For context, the same analysis also ranked **vulvovaginitis** as a prediction (score 99.59%). That finding is essentially on-label use for candidal vulvovaginitis and is supported by many trials and publications, including completed Phase 3 studies. It is not a genuine repurposing signal. A third prediction, postmenopausal atrophic vaginitis, has no supporting studies and a poor mechanistic fit.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The acne prediction rests on a model score and a single suspended combination-product trial with no results. Clotrimazole's mechanism does not address the main causes of acne, and no literature supports this use.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- A controlled trial of clotrimazole alone (not a combination) in acne, with a clear comparator
- Mechanistic or preclinical data on clotrimazole's effect on acne-relevant targets (sebum, *C. acnes*, *Malassezia*, inflammation)
- Package insert warnings and contraindications for the topical forms that would be used
- A review of the knowledge-graph-flagged conditions, especially skin atrophy and telangiectasis, which matter for long-term facial use
