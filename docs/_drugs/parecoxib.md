## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the compiled drug record. Based on known pharmacology, parecoxib is a prodrug of valdecoxib, a selective COX-2 inhibitor with anti-inflammatory and analgesic properties.

COX-2-driven prostaglandin (PGE2) synthesis contributes to neurogenic inflammation, trigeminal sensitization and CGRP release in migraine. Inhibiting COX-2 is therefore mechanistically plausible for treating an acute attack. Preclinical work is consistent with this: parecoxib affected plasma protein extravasation and c-fos expression in a rat model of trigeminovascular activation, and IL-1β was shown to stimulate COX-2-dependent PGE2 synthesis and CGRP release in rat trigeminal ganglia cells.

The TxGNN score is very high, but it is a model prediction only. The single clinical paper is a pilot comparison of intravenous parecoxib 40 mg, oral rizatriptan and subcutaneous sumatriptan. Its design and size cannot be verified from the available information, and it may be small or non-randomized. The data do not support an evidence level above L3.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The only clinical support is a single pilot-type study of unverified design, with no registered trials. The remaining evidence is preclinical, and the high TxGNN score is a prediction only. The other predicted indications (migraine with brainstem aura, migraine susceptibility, pulmonary hypertension) have weaker evidence, and for pulmonary hypertension potential harm cannot be excluded. Migraine-related predictions should be consolidated under migraine disorder.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings, contraindications and approved indications from the Indian licences
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Full review of the 2011 pilot study's design, sample size and outcomes
- Randomized controlled trial evidence for parecoxib in acute migraine
- A cardiovascular risk assessment and a safety plan for repeated or non-surgical use
- Confirmation of the injectable route's practicality for migraine
