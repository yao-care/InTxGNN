## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available for this report. Based on general knowledge, miltefosine is an alkylphosphocholine compound with activity against Leishmania parasites. Its effects are thought to involve disruption of lipid metabolism and membrane signalling. The data compiled for this report also lists AKT1 as a pharmacological target.

Leishmaniasis is a protozoan infection, while *Echinococcus granulosus* is a tapeworm (cestode) that causes cystic echinococcosis. Both are parasitic diseases, so a shared vulnerability in parasite membrane or lipid signalling is conceivable. Whether miltefosine acts on *Echinococcus granulosus* has not been verified in the data compiled here. This is a hypothesis generated by the model, not a demonstrated link.

Two other predictions were also generated: alveolar echinococcosis (score 0.9921) and smouldering systemic mastocytosis (score 0.9905). Both likewise have no direct supporting studies of miltefosine. The only publication retrieved for alveolar echinococcosis tested other drugs (itraconazole, methiazole, nitazoxanide) in vitro and does not evaluate miltefosine. The link to mastocytosis is biologically unclear.
