# Miltefosine: From Leishmaniasis to Diffuse Cutaneous Leishmaniasis

## One-Sentence Summary

Miltefosine is an oral alkylphosphocholine drug originally used to treat leishmaniasis, including visceral and mucocutaneous forms.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Diffuse Cutaneous Leishmaniasis**,
with **no registered clinical trials** but **20 publications** (mostly case reports, reviews and susceptibility studies) currently supporting this direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, miltefosine is an alkylphosphocholine with direct antileishmanial activity. Its efficacy in visceral, cutaneous and mucocutaneous leishmaniasis has been established, and it may mechanistically be applicable to diffuse cutaneous leishmaniasis. Pharmacology data also list AKT1 (a signaling kinase) as a molecular target.

Diffuse cutaneous leishmaniasis is caused by the same genus of parasites as the diseases miltefosine is already used for. It is rare and difficult to treat, and is associated with absent specific cell-mediated immunity and a heavy parasite burden. The link between the original and new indication is therefore direct: it is the same type of infection in a different clinical form.

Clinical experience is encouraging but limited. Case reports describe clearance of lesions during treatment, but relapse after stopping, which limits confidence in durable efficacy.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications (knowledge-graph associations, not label text)**: Sjogren-Larsson syndrome; congenital ichthyosiform erythroderma.
- **Drug Interactions**: 9 moderate-level interactions were identified, mainly with hormonal contraceptives and related agents: levonorgestrel, norethisterone, desogestrel, ethinylestradiol, drospirenone, norgestrel, dienogest and norgestimate. A moderate interaction with idelalisib was also found. This is clinically relevant because contraception is required during miltefosine use.
- **Other guardrails noted in the evidence review**: teratogenicity (effective contraception required), and gastrointestinal, hepatic and renal toxicity.

Please also refer to the package insert for full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Miltefosine is already marketed in India and approved for other forms of leishmaniasis, and case reports and susceptibility data show activity in diffuse cutaneous leishmaniasis. However, evidence is limited to case reports, small studies and reviews, and relapse after an initial response limits confidence in durable efficacy.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Controlled clinical studies in diffuse cutaneous leishmaniasis (none are currently registered)
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the Indian label
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- A pregnancy-prevention and contraception plan, given teratogenicity and the interactions with hormonal contraceptives
- Monitoring of gastrointestinal, hepatic and renal toxicity
- Prolonged follow-up for relapse
