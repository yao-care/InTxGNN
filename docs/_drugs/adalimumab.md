# Adalimumab: From Crohn Disease and Rheumatoid Arthritis to Rheumatoid Vasculitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Adalimumab is an anti-TNF-alpha antibody, originally used for Crohn disease, rheumatoid arthritis, psoriatic arthritis, ankylosing spondylitis, ulcerative colitis and several other inflammatory conditions.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **rheumatoid vasculitis**, but the supporting evidence is thin: **1 clinical trial** (which does not test efficacy) and **only case reports, reviews and observational reports** in the literature. Some of these reports describe vasculitis arising *during* anti-TNF therapy.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Adalimumab is a fully human monoclonal antibody that neutralizes TNF-alpha. TNF-alpha is a key driver of the systemic inflammation behind rheumatoid arthritis, and adalimumab's efficacy in that disease is well established.

Rheumatoid vasculitis is an extra-articular complication of rheumatoid arthritis. The link is therefore plausible: the same inflammatory pathway that adalimumab blocks in joint disease could contribute to blood-vessel inflammation.

The evidence is much weaker than this logic suggests. Support comes from isolated case reports. One describes digital vasculitis in a rheumatoid arthritis patient who responded well to adalimumab. Another describes an acute pulmonary hypertension crisis in a patient with rheumatoid vasculitis after the adalimumab dose was reduced.

Against this, anti-TNF agents have also been linked to paradoxical autoimmune events, including vasculitis, antiphospholipid syndrome and ANCA-associated nephritis. The prediction is therefore mechanistically sensible but unproven and potentially double-edged.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions:** 353 interactions were found.
  - *Major*:
    - Systemic and local corticosteroids (betamethasone, budesonide, dexamethasone, hydrocortisone, prednisolone, prednisone, triamcinolone including ophthalmic).
    - Deferiprone.
    - Radiopharmaceutical or radioimmunotherapy agents (ibritumomab tiuxetan, iobenguane I-131, tositumomab, tositumomab I-131).
  - *Moderate*: metronidazole, tinidazole, rosuvastatin, simvastatin.
  - *Minor*: zinc salts (acetate, gluconate, sulfate).
- **Class-related safety signals from the literature:**
  - Risk of infection, including tuberculosis reactivation.
  - Paradoxical autoimmune events: vasculitis, antiphospholipid syndrome, lupus-like reactions and ANCA-associated nephritis.
  - Immunogenicity (anti-drug antibodies).

Package insert warnings and contraindications were not available in the data compiled for this report. Please refer to the package insert for them.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The TxGNN score is very high (99.80%) and the mechanism is plausible, but the evidence is limited to case reports and observational reports, and the single registered trial does not test efficacy. Some reports also link adalimumab to new-onset vasculitis, so benefit versus harm in rheumatoid vasculitis is unresolved.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Controlled or at least systematic cohort data on adalimumab for rheumatoid vasculitis, including comparison with standard care such as rituximab or cyclophosphamide.
- Analysis of whether anti-TNF-associated paradoxical vasculitis could confound or offset any benefit.
- The India package insert warnings and contraindications, and a safety plan covering infection and TB screening, autoimmune events and corticosteroid co-use.
- Detailed mechanism of action data to strengthen the mechanistic link analysis.
