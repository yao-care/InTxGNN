## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, lomustine is a lipophilic nitrosourea alkylating agent. It damages DNA, and its lipophilicity lets it cross the blood-brain barrier. This property is why it is used in central nervous system tumours and why it is of interest in lymphoid cancers that involve the brain.

Lymphomas are generally sensitive to alkylating agents. Oral lomustine-containing regimens, such as combinations with procarbazine, etoposide and cyclophosphamide, have been tested in AIDS-related non-Hodgkin lymphoma and primary CNS lymphoma. These studies concern lymphoma subtypes treated with multi-drug regimens, so they support the mechanistic plausibility but do not directly show that lomustine works in lymphosarcoma.

The only study that directly names lymphosarcoma is a mouse study of intracranially transplanted lymphosarcoma. Lomustine extended survival 1.6-fold on its own and 3.3-fold when combined with gemcitabine. Veterinary data in canine T-cell lymphoma add supportive but non-human evidence. This is why the evidence level is capped at L4.

## Safety Considerations

- **Conditions flagged as contraindicated or of concern:** kidney disease, thrombocytopenia, leukopenia, hyperbilirubinemia (including Gilbert syndrome), drug-induced liver injury, pulmonary fibrosis, and active infectious or fungal disease. These come from relation data, not label text.
- **Drug Interactions:** 172 interactions were identified. Major ones include cimetidine, deferiprone, adalimumab and samarium (153Sm) lexidronam. Moderate ones include mercaptopurine, zidovudine, aldesleukin, alemtuzumab and chloramphenicol. Because of the overlapping myelosuppression and immunosuppression, co-medications need careful review.

Package insert warnings and contraindications for the Indian products are not available in the compiled data. Please refer to the package insert.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The evidence level is L4. The only study directly addressing lymphosarcoma is a mouse study. The human trials are small, mostly terminated, withdrawn or very small, and cover related lymphoma subtypes in multi-drug regimens, so the specific contribution of lomustine cannot be separated out.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Human studies that directly address lymphosarcoma with lomustine
- Mechanism of action data
- Package insert warnings and contraindications for the Indian products
- A myelosuppression monitoring and interaction management plan
