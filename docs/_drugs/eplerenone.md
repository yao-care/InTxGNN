## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, eplerenone is a selective mineralocorticoid receptor (MR) antagonist. Its efficacy in hypertension is established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to pulmonary hypertension.

MR activation contributes to vascular remodelling, fibrosis and inflammation, which could plausibly be relevant to pulmonary vascular disease. Both hypertension and pulmonary hypertension involve abnormal vascular tone and remodelling, which is the likely basis for the model's prediction.

However, the only support for this exact prediction is the high TxGNN score. The link is computational and unverified.

Related indirect evidence exists for a neighbouring prediction, **pulmonary hypertension owing to lung disease and/or hypoxia** (same score, 99.50%). It has 14 retrieved publications, mostly preclinical, covering:
- Hypoxia-driven MR activation, with eplerenone reducing renal fibrosis and atrial remodelling in rats
- Aldosterone promoting pulmonary vascular fibrosis and abnormal smooth muscle cell survival
- Eplerenone attenuating pulmonary vascular remodelling in a pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) model
- Mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist treatment improving interventricular dependence in a rat PAH model

These studies support a hypoxia–MR–remodelling axis but do not show benefit on pulmonary arterial pressure or clinical outcomes in humans. Several retrieved papers are off-target (choroidal blood flow, liver cirrhosis, perivascular fat, renoprotection). Evidence for that neighbouring prediction is graded L4 (preclinical), and it is framed as a research question.
