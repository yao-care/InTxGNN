# Norethisterone: From Endometriosis and Ovarian Failure to Amenorrhea

## One-Sentence Summary

Norethisterone is a synthetic progestin originally used for conditions such as endometriosis, primary ovarian failure, and postmenopausal symptoms.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **amenorrhea**,
but there are currently **no registered clinical trials** and only **20 publications**, mostly about contraceptive use, so the evidence is limited to model prediction and indirect literature.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, norethisterone is a synthetic progestin that acts as a progesterone receptor agonist. Its efficacy in gynaecological conditions such as endometriosis and ovarian failure is established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to amenorrhea.

In secondary amenorrhea where the endometrium has been primed by oestrogen but progesterone is insufficient, a course of progestin followed by withdrawal bleeding is a well-established clinical approach (the progestin challenge test). This is biologically plausible and consistent with the drug's known endometrial effects. The predicted indication also sits close to the existing endometriosis and ovarian failure uses, which supports plausibility.

However, this is indirect support. The retrieved literature mostly concerns contraceptive use, where amenorrhea appears as a side effect or bleeding-pattern outcome rather than a treatment target. The high TxGNN score is a computational prediction, not clinical evidence.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The TxGNN score is high and the progestin-challenge use in secondary amenorrhea is biologically plausible, but there are no clinical trials for this indication and the literature mainly describes amenorrhea as a contraceptive side effect. Evidence remains at the model-prediction level (L4).

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Clinical evidence directly evaluating norethisterone for treating amenorrhea (for example, studies of progestin withdrawal in secondary amenorrhea)
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- India package insert warnings and contraindications, to allow safety screening
- Review of the knowledge-graph contraindications (hypertension, liver disease, diabetes, and others) against the intended patient population
- Confirmation of the approved indications in the India licences, which currently list no indication text
