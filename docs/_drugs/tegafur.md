# Tegafur: From Gastric Cancer to Colonic Neoplasm

## One-Sentence Summary

Tegafur is an oral prodrug of 5-fluorouracil (5-FU), originally used to treat gastric cancer.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Colonic Neoplasm**,
with **16 clinical trials** and **20 publications** currently supporting this direction, including several completed Phase 3 trials of tegafur-containing regimens.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, tegafur is a fluoropyrimidine prodrug. It is converted mainly by hepatic CYP2A6 into 5-FU, which inhibits thymidylate synthase and is incorporated into RNA and DNA. In the UFT combination, uracil blocks DPD-mediated breakdown of 5-FU and raises its exposure.

Gastric cancer and colonic neoplasm are both gastrointestinal adenocarcinomas, and fluoropyrimidines are a backbone of treatment for colorectal cancer. The link is therefore direct and biologically well established.

One caveat: the positive evidence comes from tegafur-containing regimens (UFT/LV, S-1) and may not generalise to tegafur used as a single agent.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Several large, completed Phase 3 trials of tegafur-containing regimens (UFT/LV) in colon and colorectal cancer, together with randomised and cohort publications, give strong evidence and a direct mechanistic link. The guardrails are needed because the evidence comes from combination regimens (UFT/LV, S-1) rather than tegafur alone, and because safety label data are not yet available.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications for the three marketed Indian products (required before safety screening can proceed)
- Approved indication text for the Indian licences
- Detailed mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Confirmation that results from UFT/LV and S-1 regimens apply to the single-agent tegafur products marketed in India
- A safety monitoring plan, including checks for DPD deficiency and fluoropyrimidine toxicity

The other nine predicted indications (for example cecum villous adenoma and colon lipoma) have little or no supporting evidence and should stay on hold. Rectosigmoid junction neoplasm is a research question and is largely covered by the colonic neoplasm evidence.
