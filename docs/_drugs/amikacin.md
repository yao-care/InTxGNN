## Amikacin: From Bacterial Infections to Paratyphoid Fever
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Amikacin is an aminoglycoside antibiotic originally used for serious Gram-negative and other bacterial infections, such as bacterial pneumonia, Pseudomonas aeruginosa infection and bacterial meningitis.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **paratyphoid fever**, but the evidence is thin: **no clinical trials** are registered and the **8 publications** retrieved are almost entirely epidemiology, antibiogram data and case reports.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the compiled data. Based on known information, amikacin is an aminoglycoside that inhibits the bacterial 30S ribosomal subunit and has activity against Gram-negative bacteria. Its efficacy in Gram-negative infections is established, and *Salmonella* Paratyphi is a Gram-negative Enterobacterales pathogen, so activity is biologically plausible.

There are important limits. *Salmonella* is an intracellular pathogen, and aminoglycosides penetrate cells poorly, which restricts their usefulness in enteric fever. The retrieved literature does not show controlled evidence of amikacin efficacy in paratyphoid fever. The high TxGNN score mostly reflects similarity to other antibacterial uses.

The closely related prediction of typhoid fever (rank 4) has somewhat stronger support. An in vitro study (PMID 19554971) found 464 *S.* Typhi isolates sensitive to amikacin and gentamicin. Amikacin has also appeared in case reports as part of combination regimens. However, a small clinical study (PMID 2598731) reported a clinical effective rate of only 36.4% with amikacin (11 patients) versus 100% with ofloxacin. Amikacin might at most be a salvage option for extensively drug-resistant isolates, which would need clinical validation.

## Safety Considerations

- **Disease-level cautions (from the knowledge graph, not label text)**: the following conditions are linked to amikacin as contraindications or cautions: kidney disease, pyoureter, hypercalcemia, nephrocalcinosis, phosphorus metabolism disease, central nervous system origin vertigo, myasthenia gravis (including adult-onset), neuromuscular junction disease, and parkinsonian disorders including Parkinson disease.
- **Drug Interactions**: 212 interactions were identified. The major-level interactions in the data compiled for this report are **magnesium sulfate** and **mannitol**. Moderate interactions include other aminoglycosides (kanamycin, neomycin), amphotericin B formulations, acetylsalicylic acid, mesalazine and related drugs, and several proton pump inhibitors (omeprazole, esomeprazole, lansoprazole, pantoprazole, rabeprazole).

Please refer to the package insert for key warnings and contraindications, as label text was not available.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The paratyphoid fever prediction has no clinical trials and only epidemiology, surveillance and case-level literature, with no controlled evidence of amikacin efficacy. Poor intracellular penetration of aminoglycosides, together with their nephrotoxicity and ototoxicity risks, argues against advancing this indication, and established alternatives exist for enteric fever.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Controlled or comparative clinical data on amikacin in enteric fever, ideally focused on drug-resistant isolates
- Review of the related typhoid fever prediction (rank 4, evidence L3), which is a stronger candidate for a research question than paratyphoid fever alone
