# Polymyxin B: From Gram-Negative Bacterial Infection to Bronchitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Polymyxin B is a polypeptide antibiotic that acts against Gram-negative bacteria. In India it is marketed as injectable and ophthalmic products.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **bronchitis**, but **no clinical trials are registered** for this use. The **12 publications** found are mostly small cohort studies, older provocation studies and animal models, so direct support is weak.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on general knowledge, polymyxin B is a bactericidal polypeptide antibiotic that disrupts the outer membrane of Gram-negative bacteria. This includes multidrug-resistant *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* and *Acinetobacter baumannii*. It is therefore mechanistically plausible for bacterial infections of the airways caused by resistant Gram-negative organisms.

The link between the original use (Gram-negative infection) and bronchitis is indirect. The relevant literature covers two areas:
- **Respiratory infection:** ventilator-associated pneumonia and tracheobronchitis comparisons (2013) and an inhaled salvage-treatment report (2007).
- **Provocation studies:** older work from 1970 to 1985 that used inhaled polymyxin B as a **bronchial provocation agent** in asthma and chronic bronchitis. Several records show it can trigger bronchial reactivity and bronchoconstriction. Animal models even use it to induce eosinophilic bronchitis.

The very high TxGNN score is a model prediction only. No bronchitis-specific efficacy trial supports it. The existing evidence also raises a safety concern about inhaled use.

*Other predictions:* the model also ranks conjunctivitis (99.06%) and laryngotracheitis (99.62%). Conjunctivitis has randomized trials, but they test the polymyxin B/trimethoprim combination, and it is an existing marketed ophthalmic use rather than true repurposing. Laryngotracheitis has no supporting trials or literature.

## Safety Considerations

- **Respiratory risk with inhalation:** the retrieved literature shows inhaled polymyxin B can provoke bronchial reactivity and bronchoconstriction, particularly in people with asthma or chronic bronchitis.
- **Systemic toxicity:** systemic use carries a risk of nephrotoxicity and neurotoxicity.
- **Drug Interactions:** 73 interactions were identified. Major-level interactions include:
  - Aminoglycosides (amikacin)
  - Neuromuscular agents (cisatracurium, botulinum toxin type A, botulinum neurotoxin A/B immune globulin)
  - Nephrotoxic agents (cidofovir, deferasirox, iodinated contrast agents such as iothalamic acid and diatrizoate)
  - Human immunoglobulin G (intravenous) and human cytomegalovirus immune globulin
  - Live cholera vaccine (*Vibrio cholerae* CVD 103-HgR)

  Moderate-level interactions include ethinylestradiol, estradiol, mestranol, mesalazine, balsalazide, ibandronate, capreomycin, clofarabine and picosulfuric acid.

Please refer to the package insert for full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The 99.87% score is a prediction only. There are no registered trials, and the literature is limited to small cohort studies of resistant Gram-negative respiratory infection, older provocation studies, and animal models. The inhalation-related bronchospasm signal and systemic toxicity must be resolved before further investment.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the India regulator's website, which are not yet available and block safety screening
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Direct clinical evidence in bacterial bronchitis, ideally a controlled trial
- A safety assessment of any inhaled or nebulized route, including bronchospasm risk, as no such formulation is registered
- A defined target population, since most bronchitis is viral or non-bacterial and a stewardship plan would be needed
