## One-Sentence Summary

Axitinib is an oral VEGFR-1/2/3 inhibitor, originally used to treat renal cell carcinoma (RCC), including clear cell RCC.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **renal cell carcinoma associated with Xp11.2 translocations/TFE3 gene fusions**.
Evidence for this subtype is thin: **1 small Phase 2 clinical trial (15 patients, still ongoing)** and **no subtype-specific publications**.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Axitinib is known as a potent, selective inhibitor of VEGF receptors 1-3, and it is established in advanced RCC. It blocks the blood-vessel growth that RCC tumours depend on.

Xp11.2/TFE3 translocation RCC is a rare subtype driven by a TFE3 gene fusion. Its downstream signalling includes activation of the VEGF and MET pathways. Because it shares this angiogenesis-driven biology with the more common clear cell RCC, VEGFR inhibition is biologically plausible.

The case for efficacy rests mainly on extrapolation from clear cell RCC. Large Phase 3 trials such as AXIS and KEYNOTE-426 support axitinib in that setting. Subtype-specific data are limited to one small ongoing trial. The high TxGNN score reflects network proximity to other RCC terms and is not independent clinical proof.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions are recorded, and axitinib is affected by CYP3A4-related interactions. The main ones are:
  - **Major**: clarithromycin, dexamethasone
  - **Moderate**: aprepitant, clotrimazole, miconazole, naltrexone
  - **Minor**: acid-reducing agents and antacids (e.g., famotidine, ranitidine, rabeprazole, esomeprazole, lansoprazole, cimetidine, aluminium hydroxide, calcium carbonate, magnesium salts)
- **Monitoring suggested for the RCC use**: hypertension, hepatotoxicity, proteinuria and haemorrhage.
- **Pediatric use**: Pediatric dosing and safety for axitinib are not established. This matters because TFE3 translocation RCC also occurs in children.

Please refer to the package insert for key warnings and contraindications, as they were not available in the compiled data.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The mechanism is plausible and axitinib is well established in other RCC settings. For this rare subtype, however, the only evidence is one ongoing 15-patient Phase 2 trial with no results and no subtype-specific literature. The case rests on extrapolation, so it is best treated as a research question until data mature.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Results from NCT03595124, or other subtype-specific efficacy data
- Detailed mechanism of action data, especially how axitinib acts on TFE3-fusion-driven signalling
- India package insert warnings and contraindications, so safety screening can be completed
- Dosing and safety evidence for paediatric and young-adult patients, if those groups are considered
- A CYP3A4 drug-interaction management plan for combination regimens
