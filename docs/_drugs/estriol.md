## Estriol: From Menopausal Hormone Therapy to Amenorrhea
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Estriol is a weak estrogen receptor agonist marketed in India as oral tablets and vaginal creams. It has long been used in Europe and Asia for post-menopausal hot flashes. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **amenorrhea**, with **no registered clinical trials** and **6 publications** (only 2 directly relevant) currently supporting this direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the compiled data. Pharmacology data shows that estriol binds estrogen receptor alpha (ESR1) and estrogen receptor beta (ESR2). It is a weak estrogen agonist.

The predicted indication is amenorrhea, particularly functional hypothalamic amenorrhea (FHA). In FHA, the hypothalamus releases GnRH in an impaired pattern. This leaves the body in a low-estrogen state with reduced ovarian function. Low-dose estrogen replacement may act as a neuroendocrine modulator. It may help restore feedback on LH secretion and support endometrial and bone health.

Two publications support this idea. A 2012 study (PMID 22137494) reports that estriol administration modulates LH secretion in women with FHA. A 2023 review (PMID 37371858) proposes a similar putative mechanism for low-dose estrogens. The high TxGNN score is consistent with this rationale, but it is a computational prediction, not clinical evidence. Mechanistic plausibility is good. However, no controlled data show that estriol restores menses or ovulation.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction is biologically plausible and has a high model score. However, direct evidence is limited to one small physiological study and one review, with no registered clinical trials and no controlled efficacy data. Package insert safety information is also missing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the India regulatory source
- Approved indication text for the Indian licences
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Controlled clinical studies of estriol in amenorrhea (especially FHA), with menses and ovulation outcomes
- Route compatibility assessment: the Indian products are oral tablets and vaginal creams, and the route best suited to amenorrhea needs to be determined
