# Olaparib: From Ovarian Cancer to Female Breast Carcinoma

## One-Sentence Summary

Olaparib is an oral PARP inhibitor, originally used to treat ovarian cancer, particularly BRCA-mutated disease.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **female breast carcinoma**,
with **30 clinical trials** and **20 publications** currently supporting this direction, including several phase 3 randomized trials (OlympiAD, OlympiA) in BRCA-mutated breast cancer.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on general pharmacological knowledge, olaparib inhibits PARP enzymes, which repair DNA single-strand breaks. In tumours with defective homologous recombination repair, such as those with germline BRCA1/2 mutations, blocking PARP leads to trapped PARP on DNA, replication fork collapse and unrepairable double-strand breaks. This "synthetic lethality" kills the tumour cells.

BRCA1/2 mutations drive both hereditary ovarian cancer and hereditary breast cancer. The same biological vulnerability that makes olaparib effective in BRCA-mutated ovarian cancer therefore applies to BRCA-mutated breast cancer. Phase 2 data (TBCRC 048) also suggest activity in tumours with other homologous recombination gene alterations such as PALB2, although this evidence is less mature.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Multiple phase 3 randomized trials (OlympiAD in metastatic disease and OlympiA in early disease) support olaparib in germline BRCA-mutated, HER2-negative breast cancer. Olaparib is already marketed in India, and a completed phase 4 study in Indian patients included BRCA-mutated metastatic breast cancer. The guardrails reflect that the evidence is strongest for germline BRCA1/2-mutated disease, while benefit in other breast cancer subtypes is less mature.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the India label, which are currently missing and block formal safety screening
- Confirmation of the approved indications on the two Indian registrations
- Official mechanism of action data to replace the general pharmacological description used here
- A patient selection plan restricted to germline BRCA1/2-mutated (and possibly other homologous recombination-deficient) breast cancer, with biomarker testing
- A monitoring plan for haematological toxicity and a review of interacting co-medications
