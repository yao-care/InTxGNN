## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the compiled data. Based on known class pharmacology, citalopram is a selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor that raises serotonin availability in the brain.

Obsessive-compulsive disorder is known to respond to serotonin reuptake inhibition. Other SSRIs, such as fluoxetine (used as the comparator in one of the listed trials), are established treatments for it. Citalopram shares this mechanism, which is consistent with the high TxGNN score.

One caution: the larger trials listed below support the SSRI class and combined drug-plus-behavioural treatment strategies in obsessive-compulsive disorder, not citalopram specifically. Citalopram-specific data come mainly from small and open-label studies.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindication-related conditions** (from drug-disease records, not label text): long QT syndrome (including familial), torsades de pointes, epilepsy, bipolar disorder, major affective disorder, liver disease, gallbladder disease, potassium deficiency, blood coagulation disease, heart disease, cardiovascular disease, neuroleptic malignant syndrome, syndrome of inappropriate ADH secretion, and serotonin syndrome.
- **Drug Interactions**: 414 interactions are recorded. Major-level examples include bupropion, omeprazole, cimetidine, cisapride, clarithromycin, lorcaserin, dexfenfluramine, diethylpropion and dolasetron. Several of these (cisapride, clarithromycin, dolasetron) fit the QT-prolongation concern above. Moderate examples include acetylsalicylic acid, morphine, famotidine and loperamide.

Please also refer to the package insert for full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Citalopram has been tested directly in obsessive-compulsive disorder, but only in one small single-blind comparison and several open-label studies. The larger trials support the SSRI class rather than citalopram specifically (Level L2). The mechanism is plausible, and the drug is widely marketed in India.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- A randomized, placebo-controlled trial of citalopram in obsessive-compulsive disorder
- Package insert warnings and contraindications for the Indian products
- A cardiac (QT) risk review and a plan to screen interacting drugs
- Dosing and safety review for paediatric use, given that the citalopram data in young patients are open-label only
