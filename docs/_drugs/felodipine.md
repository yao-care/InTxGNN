## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, felodipine is a vasoselective dihydropyridine L-type calcium channel blocker. It lowers blood pressure by relaxing vascular smooth muscle and reducing systemic vascular resistance. This vasodilatory action makes a pulmonary vasodilator effect plausible.

However, pulmonary hypertension is a heterogeneous group of conditions. Calcium channel blockers are appropriate only for a small vasoreactive subset. The high TxGNN score is a computational prediction only, and no trials or literature were found for this specific indication.

Other predicted indications fall into two groups:
- **Close to the original indication:** malignant renovascular hypertension and malignant hypertensive renal disease. These are effectively extensions of felodipine's antihypertensive use rather than true repurposing, and no trials or literature support them.
- **Better supported:** **Prinzmetal angina**, driven by coronary vasospasm, for which calcium channel blockers are already a standard drug class. Felodipine's main incremental value there would be once-daily dosing. **Chronic pulmonary heart disease** has small, older haemodynamic studies, and pulmonary hypertension owing to lung disease and/or hypoxia has only indirect preclinical studies.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions:** 246 interactions were identified. Most are rated Moderate, including hydrocortisone, bupropion, aprepitant, triamcinolone, morphine, acetylsalicylic acid, dexamethasone, cimetidine and clarithromycin. **Dolasetron** is rated **Major**.
- **Disease-related cautions (from the drug-disease knowledge graph, not label text):** The graph links felodipine with caution in conditions including hypotensive disorder, aortic stenosis, hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, coronary artery disease, myocardial infarction, kidney disease, liver disease, angioedema, and neutropenia/agranulocytosis. These should be checked against the package insert.
- **Indication-specific concerns:** In pulmonary hypertension due to lung disease, calcium channel blockers can blunt hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction and worsen ventilation-perfusion matching and gas exchange. Some vasodilators have also produced negative outcomes in heart failure.

Warnings and contraindications from the package insert are not available. Please refer to the package insert for full safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The top predicted indication, pulmonary hypertension, rests on a computational score alone (L5) with no trials or literature. Calcium channel blockers suit only a vasoreactive subset, and there is a plausible safety signal in lung disease. The best-supported prediction is Prinzmetal angina (L2), but calcium channel blockers are already standard there, so the repurposing value is limited to once-daily dosing convenience.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications for the marketed Indian products (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- For pulmonary hypertension: controlled clinical data in a defined vasoreactive subgroup, plus an assessment of the gas-exchange risk in hypoxic lung disease
- For Prinzmetal angina: confirmation of study design and phase for the felodipine studies, and a decision on whether the once-daily convenience justifies a new indication
- Approved indication text for the Indian registrations
