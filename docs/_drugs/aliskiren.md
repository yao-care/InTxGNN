## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Aliskiren is a direct renin inhibitor. It blocks the first, rate-limiting step of the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS), which lowers angiotensin II production. Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report, so this description reflects the drug's known class.

Hypertension and pulmonary hypertension are both vascular pressure disorders, and RAAS activation may contribute to pulmonary vascular remodelling. Animal studies show that intermittent hypoxia drives angiotensin II-mediated effects. Aliskiren reduced hypoxia-induced atrial remodelling in rats (PMID 32982180), and intermittent hypoxia causes sympathoexcitation through circulating angiotensin II (PMID 29645283).

These findings support RAAS involvement in hypoxia-related pathology. However, they come from animal and in vitro models, mostly address atrial remodelling or autonomic effects rather than pulmonary vascular disease, and include no human data. The high TxGNN score is likely driven by overlap with the approved hypertension indication.

## Safety Considerations

Safety information from the knowledge graph (not package insert text) lists the following diseases as contraindication-related for aliskiren:

- **Angioedema**: including hereditary, acquired, and RAAS-blocker-induced forms
- **Liver and biliary disease**: liver failure, liver disease, hepatic coma/encephalopathy, pancreatitis, gallbladder disease, neonatal jaundice
- **Renal and electrolyte disorders**: impaired renal function, anuria, renal artery obstruction, potassium deficiency, hypercalcaemia, nephrocalcinosis, phosphorus metabolism disease, hyperparathyroidism, osteitis fibrosa
- **Metabolic disorders**: diabetes mellitus, diabetic ketoacidosis, gout, hyperuricaemia
- **Haematological disorders**: neutropenia, agranulocytosis
- **Other**: hypotensive disorder, systemic lupus erythematosus (including paediatric)

**Drug interactions:** 114 interactions were identified; those listed are all moderate severity. Examples include:
- Corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, prednisolone, prednisone, dexamethasone, betamethasone, budesonide)
- SGLT2 inhibitors (canagliflozin, dapagliflozin, empagliflozin, ertugliflozin)
- Potassium supplements (potassium chloride, bicarbonate, citrate, gluconate)
- Aspirin, aprepitant, clarithromycin, clotrimazole, miconazole, eliglustat

Literature from the ALTITUDE trial also raised concerns about combining aliskiren with ACE inhibitors or ARBs in diabetic patients (hypotension, stroke, hyperkalaemia, renal events). Please refer to the package insert for full safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The TxGNN score is very high, but there are no clinical trials and the supporting literature is entirely preclinical and mostly indirect, with no pulmonary vascular data. The prediction is likely driven by overlap with the approved hypertension indication.

**Summary of other predicted indications:**

Cerebrovascular disorder has the strongest support: animal studies show neuroprotection (PMIDs 21124781, 27180190, 21859961), and trial NCT01417104 (ALPINE, Phase 2/3, terminated, 71 participants, surrogate MRI plaque endpoint) tested aliskiren in atherosclerosis. Human evidence for stroke treatment is indirect.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data and package insert warnings and contraindications (currently unavailable)
- Preclinical studies in pulmonary vascular models (e.g. hypoxia-induced pulmonary hypertension) to test the RAAS hypothesis directly
- A safety plan addressing the contraindication signals above, especially angioedema, renal impairment, hyperkalaemia, and combination use with ACE inhibitors/ARBs in diabetic patients
- For cerebrovascular disorder: a prospective trial with clinical stroke or cognitive endpoints
