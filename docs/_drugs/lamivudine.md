# Lamivudine: From Chronic Hepatitis B and HIV Infection to Simian Immunodeficiency Virus Infection

## One-Sentence Summary

Lamivudine is a nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitor originally used to treat chronic hepatitis B virus infection, HIV infection and AIDS.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **simian immunodeficiency virus infection**, but the supporting evidence is limited to **0 clinical trials** and **animal and laboratory publications** only.
This is an animal-model disease closely related to HIV, so it is not a true human repurposing opportunity.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, lamivudine is a nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitor (NRTI). Its efficacy in HIV-1 and hepatitis B has been established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to simian immunodeficiency virus (SIV).

SIV is a close lentiviral relative of HIV with a homologous reverse transcriptase, so activity against it is plausible. Macaque studies support this indirectly. They cover the fitness of the M184V resistance mutation in SIV reverse transcriptase, viral decay under combination antiretroviral therapy, and post-exposure prophylaxis with zidovudine, lamivudine and indinavir.

SIV is mainly a veterinary and animal-model disease with no separate human clinical indication. The drug's existing HIV indication already covers the human equivalent. The prediction is therefore scientifically coherent but offers little new value as a repurposing opportunity.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction has only indirect preclinical support from macaque and in vitro studies, and no clinical trials. SIV is an animal-model disease already covered in practice by lamivudine's existing HIV indication, so it offers no real repurposing opportunity.

The other predicted indications are weaker:
- **Feline acquired immunodeficiency syndrome**: evidence is limited to veterinary studies, and it is outside the human repurposing scope.
- **Chronic hepatitis C virus infection**: lamivudine has no established activity against HCV, and the retrieved literature is general viral hepatitis reviews.
- **A rare neurodevelopmental disorder and obsolete familial combined hyperlipidaemia**: no literature or mechanistic link was found.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data for lamivudine
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the Indian manufacturers, which are needed before safety screening
- Approved-indication text for the Indian licences
- A clear human clinical question, since the current prediction duplicates the existing HIV indication
