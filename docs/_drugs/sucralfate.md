## Sucralfate: From Duodenal Ulcer to Duodenogastric Reflux
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Sucralfate is a mucosal protectant originally used to treat duodenal ulcer.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **duodenogastric reflux** (bile reflux gastritis), with **no registered clinical trials** but **11 publications**, including **2 randomized trials**, supporting this direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

According to the Clinical Pharmacology section of the US FDA-approved prescribing information for sucralfate: "Although the mechanism of sucralfate's ability to accelerate healing of duodenal ulcers remains to be fully defined, it is known that it exerts its effect through a local, rather than systemic, action. The following observations also appear pertinent: 1." ([DailyMed: Sucralfate Oral Suspension (sucralfate) prescribing information, Glenmark Pharmaceuticals Inc., USA](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=1e595c2a-edd0-43f7-a2db-69553b9557d9), label version 2026-01-06; accessed 2026-11-19.) Based on general pharmacological knowledge, sucralfate forms a protective, adherent barrier over damaged gastric mucosa and binds bile acids and pepsin. This mechanism underlies its established use in duodenal ulcer.

Duodenogastric reflux injures the stomach lining through bile and other duodenal contents flowing backward. Binding bile acids and shielding the mucosa could plausibly counter that injury. The literature includes randomized trials in symptomatic alkaline reflux gastritis, including post-cholecystectomy gastritis, and a case series of 50 patients with post-surgical biliary reflux gastritis.

This mechanistic link rests on general knowledge of sucralfate, not on mechanism data supplied for this report.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Two randomized trials and a case series support sucralfate in alkaline reflux gastritis, and the proposed mechanism is consistent with its known cytoprotective action. However, the trials are small and old, no registered clinical trials exist, and the label safety information is missing. This supports further study rather than a full "Go."

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- India package insert warnings and contraindications (blocking for safety screening)
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Full-text review of the two randomized trials to confirm efficacy outcomes
- A safety plan for patients with renal impairment or diabetes, and a dosing-separation plan for interacting drugs (antiretroviral integrase inhibitors, tetracyclines, bisphosphonates)
