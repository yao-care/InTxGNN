## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on general pharmacological knowledge, sucralfate forms a protective, adherent barrier over damaged gastric mucosa and binds bile acids and pepsin. This mechanism underlies its established use in duodenal ulcer.

Duodenogastric reflux injures the stomach lining through bile and other duodenal contents flowing backward. Binding bile acids and shielding the mucosa could plausibly counter that injury. The literature includes randomized trials in symptomatic alkaline reflux gastritis, including post-cholecystectomy gastritis, and a case series of 50 patients with post-surgical biliary reflux gastritis.

This mechanistic link rests on general knowledge of sucralfate, not on mechanism data supplied for this report.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Two randomized trials and a case series support sucralfate in alkaline reflux gastritis, and the proposed mechanism is consistent with its known cytoprotective action. However, the trials are small and old, no registered clinical trials exist, and the label safety information is missing. This supports further study rather than a full "Go."

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- India package insert warnings and contraindications (blocking for safety screening)
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Full-text review of the two randomized trials to confirm efficacy outcomes
- A safety plan for patients with renal impairment or diabetes, and a dosing-separation plan for interacting drugs (antiretroviral integrase inhibitors, tetracyclines, bisphosphonates)
