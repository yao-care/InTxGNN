# Lurasidone: From Bipolar Disorder and Schizophrenia to Manic Bipolar Affective Disorder

## One-Sentence Summary

Lurasidone is an atypical antipsychotic originally used to treat bipolar disorder and schizophrenia.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **manic bipolar affective disorder**, with **1 clinical trial** (withdrawn, no participants enrolled) and **20 publications** (mostly reviews, guidelines and meta-analyses) currently related to this direction.
This is not a truly novel indication, because lurasidone is already used in bipolar disorder (mainly bipolar depression).

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on general knowledge of the drug class, lurasidone is an atypical antipsychotic. Its blocking of dopamine D2 and serotonin 5-HT2A receptors, together with activity at 5-HT7 and 5-HT1A receptors, plausibly supports an antimanic effect.

Lurasidone is already used in bipolar disorder, and mania is one phase of that condition. The prediction is therefore close to the drug's existing use rather than a leap into a new disease area.

The evidence for acute mania is weaker than for bipolar depression. Most of the literature found concerns bipolar depression, and the only trial directly testing mania was withdrawn. The mechanistic link rests on general class pharmacology, not on a documented mechanism record.

## Safety Considerations

The package insert warnings and contraindications could not be retrieved, so please refer to the package insert for label safety information.

- **Disease-level contraindication signals (from the knowledge graph, not label text)**: The graph links lurasidone to a broad set of conditions of concern, including:
  - Cardiovascular and neurological: orthostatic hypotension, stroke and cerebrovascular disorders, transient ischemic attack, epilepsy, neuroleptic malignant syndrome, tardive dyskinesia
  - Metabolic and endocrine: hyperglycemia, diabetes mellitus, diabetic ketoacidosis, hyperlipidemia, hyperprolactinemia
  - Haematological, hepatic and renal: neutropenia, leukopenia, agranulocytosis, liver disease or failure, impaired renal function
- **Drug interactions**: 179 interactions were recorded. Examples classed as **Major** are bupropion, aprepitant, morphine and clarithromycin. Moderate examples include several antidiabetic agents (acarbose, metformin, pioglitazone, alogliptin, canagliflozin, chlorpropamide, albiglutide), anticholinergics (atropine, hyoscyamine, glycopyrronium), epinephrine, dexamethasone, cimetidine and lorcaserin. Strong CYP3A4 inhibitors and inducers deserve particular attention when assessing this class of interaction.
- **Metabolic monitoring**: Weight gain and glucose dysregulation with antipsychotics are discussed in the supporting literature, so metabolic monitoring is advisable.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold (treat as a research question)**

**Rationale:**
The TxGNN score is very high, but lurasidone is already used in bipolar disorder, so this prediction adds little that is new. Direct evidence for acute mania is thin: the only mania trial was withdrawn with no enrolment, and the supporting literature is mostly reviews, guidelines and meta-analyses on bipolar depression. Package-insert safety data are also missing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package-insert warnings and contraindications from the Indian regulatory sources, to enable safety screening
- Mechanism of action data (for example from DrugBank)
- Randomized controlled trial evidence specific to acute manic or mixed episodes (adult and paediatric)
- Review of the approved indication wording on the Indian labels, to confirm whether acute mania falls inside or outside the current labelling
- A metabolic, cardiovascular and drug-interaction monitoring plan
