## Loxapine: Retinal dystrophy with or without extraocular anomalies
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Loxapine is a first-generation antipsychotic originally used for schizophrenia, bipolar disorder and major affective disorder.
Because bipolar disorder is already a listed original indication, this is better described as a confirmation of an existing use than as true repurposing.

**Note on the evidence level:** No clinical trials were supplied in the data compiled for this report. The L1 grade rests on the literature, which describes two Phase 3 randomised controlled trials of inhaled loxapine, and on regulatory approval of that formulation in the USA and EU. The primary trial publications should be verified before the grade is finalised.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the compiled data. Loxapine is a dibenzoxazepine antipsychotic. It is known to block dopamine D2 and serotonin 5-HT2A receptors, which is the pharmacology used to control mania and agitation.

Mania and schizophrenia share psychotic and agitated features that respond to dopamine antagonism. Bipolar disorder is already among loxapine's original indications. An inhaled formulation (Adasuve) is approved in the USA and EU for acute agitation associated with bipolar I disorder or schizophrenia, and the literature supports this use. The prediction is therefore mechanistically sound, though it largely restates existing knowledge.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions:** 102 interactions were recorded. Major ones include **bupropion**, **morphine** and **potassium citrate**. Moderate ones include anticholinergic agents (atropine, hyoscyamine, glycopyrronium, dicyclomine, trospium), loperamide, epinephrine, dronabinol, nabilone and lorcaserin.
- **Formulation-specific guardrails (from the literature):** the inhaled formulation carries a bronchospasm risk and requires restriction to controlled settings, with exclusion of patients with asthma or COPD. Sedation, extrapyramidal symptoms and QT effects also need attention.

Package insert warnings for the Indian products were not available and should be consulted.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Loxapine's D2/5-HT2A antagonism fits the treatment of mania and agitation, and bipolar disorder is already a labelled original indication. The literature, including reviews of two Phase III trials of inhaled loxapine, supports use in acute agitation. However, no trials were supplied, and the safety burden is substantial.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Verification of the primary Phase 3 RCT publications to confirm the L1 grade
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the Indian product labels
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Confirmation of which formulations are actually available in India, since inhaled loxapine is not among the listed products
- A safety monitoring plan covering the large interaction burden, bronchospasm risk, sedation, extrapyramidal symptoms and QT effects

**Other predictions (all Hold):** The nine other predicted indications are retinal dystrophy, hydranencephaly, X-linked myopia (three entries), congenital disorder of glycosylation, Charcot-Marie-Tooth type 1G, perisylvian polymicrogyria and atypical glycine encephalopathy. For the myopia entries, D2 antagonism is expected to be unhelpful or counterproductive.
