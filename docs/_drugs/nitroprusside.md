## Nitroprusside: From Hypertensive Emergencies to Migraine Disorder
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Nitroprusside is an intravenous nitric oxide (NO) donor, marketed in India as a 50 mg injection. The approved indication text is not recorded in the data compiled for this report.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **migraine disorder**, but there are **no clinical trials** and the retrieved literature is mostly preclinical or observational.
The literature also suggests NO donors tend to *provoke* migraine rather than relieve it, so this top-ranked prediction is not supported mechanistically.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the drug record. From general pharmacology, nitroprusside releases nitric oxide, which activates soluble guanylate cyclase, raises cGMP and relaxes vascular smooth muscle.

The NO/cGMP and CGRP pathways are central to migraine biology. However, the direction of the link argues against benefit. NO donors such as nitroglycerin are established experimental migraine triggers, and sodium nitroprusside is used to *induce* migraine-like behaviour in the mouse models in the retrieved studies. The TxGNN score therefore likely reflects pathway proximity in the knowledge graph, not therapeutic potential.

For context, other predictions in the ranking fit better. **Pulmonary hypertension** (score 99.98%, evidence level L3) is mechanistically coherent with vasodilation and is supported by clinical studies and reviews. Pulmonary use is limited by systemic hypotension, ventilation-perfusion mismatch and cyanide toxicity. Hair-loss conditions (alopecia, hypotrichosis), kyphoscoliotic heart disease and benign prostatic hyperplasia have little or no direct evidence.

For warnings and contraindications, please refer to the package insert. Known concerns for the route and drug class include systemic hypotension and cyanide toxicity.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The migraine prediction has no clinical trials and only preclinical or observational support, and the evidence suggests NO donors trigger migraine rather than treat it. The high TxGNN score is not backed by clinical evidence. If any indication is pursued, pulmonary hypertension has the most coherent mechanism and clinical literature, though inhaled delivery remains investigational.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The package insert (warnings, contraindications and approved indications) from the three India registrations
- Mechanism of action data for the record
- A review of why the TxGNN migraine prediction conflicts with the known pro-migraine effect of NO donors
- If pulmonary hypertension is pursued, a focused evidence review and a plan for delivery route, haemodynamic monitoring and cyanide-toxicity risk
