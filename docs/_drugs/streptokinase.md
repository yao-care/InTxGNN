# Streptokinase: From Thrombolytic Therapy to Myocardial Infarction

## One-Sentence Summary

Streptokinase is a clot-dissolving (thrombolytic) enzyme given by injection, marketed in India under 20 valid registrations.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **myocardial infarction**, with **7 registered clinical trials** and **20 publications** retrieved for this prediction.
Note that myocardial infarction is already an established use of streptokinase, so this is better described as confirming a known use than as true repurposing.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, streptokinase forms a complex with plasminogen that converts plasminogen to plasmin. Plasmin then breaks down fibrin, the protein mesh that holds a clot together.

In a heart attack, a clot blocks a coronary artery. Dissolving that clot restores blood flow and limits damage to heart muscle. This is why the mechanism fits the predicted indication directly.

The literature includes reviews of the large randomised trials (such as ISIS-2, GISSI and ISAM), which describe a survival benefit with intravenous streptokinase. Newer fibrin-specific agents and primary PCI (percutaneous coronary intervention) are now often preferred alternatives.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: 122 interactions are recorded. Several are rated Major, mostly with other agents that affect bleeding or clotting:
  - Anticoagulants and antithrombotics: apixaban, betrixaban, bivalirudin and abciximab.
  - Other Major pairings: acalabrutinib, avapritinib, deferasirox, ibritumomab tiuxetan and tositumomab (I-131).
  - Moderate interactions include aspirin, NSAIDs (ibuprofen, ketorolac, celecoxib), aminocaproic acid, omega-3 fatty acids and ginger.
- **Guardrails from the evidence review**: bleeding and intracranial haemorrhage risk; anti-streptokinase antibodies and allergic reactions that limit repeat use; haemorrhagic infarction and septal rupture reported after thrombolysis in case-level and observational literature. Jaundice after streptokinase has also been reported in one case.

Package insert warnings and contraindications were not available in the data compiled for this report, so please refer to the package insert for full safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
The mechanism directly fits myocardial infarction, and several completed Phase 3 trials plus large randomised trial reviews support streptokinase in this setting. This is an established use rather than new repurposing, and the main concerns are bleeding, immunogenicity, and the availability of fibrin-specific agents and primary PCI.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the Indian regulator and manufacturers, which are currently missing and block formal safety screening
- Approved indication text for the Indian registrations
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank to complete the mechanistic analysis
- A bleeding-risk and re-administration (antibody) management plan
- A comparison of streptokinase with fibrin-specific agents and primary PCI in the intended care setting
