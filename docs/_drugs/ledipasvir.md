## Ledipasvir: From Chronic Hepatitis C to Hepatitis B Virus Infection
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Ledipasvir is an HCV NS5A inhibitor, used in fixed-dose combination with sofosbuvir for chronic hepatitis C virus infection.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **hepatitis B virus infection**, but direct support is thin: one single-arm Phase 2 study (**21 patients**) tests the indication, and the remaining trials and publications are mostly about HCV treatment or HBV reactivation during HCV therapy.
The mechanistic basis for an anti-HBV effect is weak, so this should be treated as a research question rather than a ready candidate.

*Note on evidence level:* The only completed Phase 2/3 study (NCT02555943, n=23) is in HCV/HBV co-infection and measures HCV cure as its primary endpoint. The only study that directly tests HBV is a single-arm Phase 2 pilot. The strict L2 label therefore overstates the strength of the evidence for HBV efficacy.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, ledipasvir is an NS5A inhibitor used with the NRTI sofosbuvir, and its efficacy in hepatitis C has been established.

The prediction is difficult to support mechanistically. HBV is a DNA hepadnavirus with a different replication machinery from HCV, and ledipasvir has no established anti-HBV activity. The high TxGNN score most likely reflects network proximity to HCV (both are hepatotropic viruses) rather than a real shared target.

Some clinical signals exist. A Phase 2 open-label study of ledipasvir/sofosbuvir for 12 weeks in HBV mono-infected subjects (NCT03312023, published as PMID 36045503) was built on retrospective observations of a modest HBsAg reduction in HBV/HCV co-infected patients. It is a small, single-arm study (n=21) and does not establish efficacy. Studies in co-infected patients mainly show that HCV is cleared, and HBV reactivation during DAA therapy is a safety concern rather than a therapeutic signal.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions:** 65 interactions were identified in total. The ones listed below are mostly Moderate, and the main ones fall into these groups:
  - Antacids (aluminum hydroxide, calcium carbonate, magaldrate, magnesium carbonate, magnesium hydroxide, magnesium oxide)
  - Acid suppressants: H2 blockers (cimetidine, famotidine, nizatidine) and proton pump inhibitors (omeprazole, esomeprazole, lansoprazole, dexlansoprazole, pantoprazole)
  - Others: eliglustat, linagliptin, loperamide and naldemedine (all Moderate), and fidaxomicin and naloxegol (both Minor)
- **HBV reactivation:** Published cohorts and case reports describe HBV reactivation in HBV/HCV co-infected or previously HBV-exposed patients treated with ledipasvir/sofosbuvir. This is the main safety issue for any use in HBV-infected patients.

Please refer to the package insert for warnings and contraindications, as this information was not available in the data compiled for this report.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Ledipasvir has no established anti-HBV mechanism, and the direct evidence is a single 21-patient, single-arm Phase 2 pilot. The other studies mainly show HCV cure or HBV reactivation risk. The high TxGNN score likely reflects network proximity to HCV rather than real HBV activity. The product is widely marketed in India, which makes access easy but does not strengthen the case for efficacy.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Detailed mechanism of action data and in vitro anti-HBV activity data for ledipasvir alone
- Full results of the HBV mono-infection pilot (PMID 36045503), followed by a controlled study with HBsAg and HBV DNA endpoints
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the Indian regulatory sources
- An HBV reactivation monitoring plan for any HBV-positive population, and a review of interactions with acid-suppressing drugs

**Other predicted indications (for reference):** Hepatitis E virus infection (L4, Hold) rests on a single case report of ribavirin-refractory infection treated with ledipasvir/sofosbuvir plus ribavirin; any effect is more plausibly due to sofosbuvir. Chronic hepatitis B virus infection (L3) has the same limitations as the lead indication. The remaining predictions (hepatitis A, Omsk hemorrhagic fever, Kyasanur forest disease, HIV infection, and animal or veterinary viral diseases) are model-only (L5) or reflect HCV treatment in other populations, and should be held.
