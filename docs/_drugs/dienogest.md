# Dienogest: From Endometriosis to Amenorrhea

## One-Sentence Summary

Dienogest is a selective progestin marketed in India as a 2 mg oral product; its approved indication text is not available in the data compiled for this report, although the literature points to endometriosis-related use.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **amenorrhea**, but **no clinical trials** and only **7 loosely related publications** are available. These publications describe amenorrhea mainly as a treatment effect rather than a therapeutic target.
This is a model-driven research question, not an established repurposing opportunity.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, dienogest is a fourth-generation progestin with selective progesterone activity, antiproliferative and endometrium-decidualizing effects, and mild antigonadotropic action. It partially suppresses ovulation.

The link to amenorrhea is largely a pharmacological *effect*. Endometriosis treatment often aims to induce amenorrhea and a hypoestrogenic environment. The high TxGNN score probably reflects this association with menstrual-pattern changes rather than a therapeutic indication. Using dienogest in secondary amenorrhea, for example as a progestin challenge or for endometrial protection in hypoestrogenic states, is plausible. However, none of the supplied evidence directly supports it.

Other top-ranked predictions are weaker. These include primary ovarian failure, isolated growth hormone deficiency, hypogonadotropic hypogonadism, fragile X carrier symptoms, BPES variants and chromosomal disorders. They have no trials or literature and are likely network-proximity artifacts. Breast fibrocystic disease (score 99.60%) has one indirect publication on breast effects and is a more plausible secondary research question.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The amenorrhea prediction has a high model score, but no clinical trials support it. The literature treats amenorrhea as an expected effect of dienogest rather than a condition it treats, so the evidence is only L4. The other top predictions have no evidence at all.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the India regulator (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Approved indication text for the registered products, to confirm the original indication
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank
- A clear definition of the intended use (for example secondary amenorrhea or progestin challenge), followed by a targeted literature search and trial design
- Further assessment of breast fibrocystic disease as a secondary research question, supported by controlled data
