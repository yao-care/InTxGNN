# Bromfenac: From Postoperative Ocular Inflammation to Eye Disease

## One-Sentence Summary

Bromfenac is a topical non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID) eye drop, marketed in India in ophthalmic formulations.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the compiled drug record. Based on known information, bromfenac is a topical NSAID that inhibits cyclooxygenase (COX-1/COX-2) and reduces prostaglandin E2 synthesis in ocular tissues.

Prostaglandins are major mediators of ocular inflammation and pain. This makes the mechanism relevant to postoperative inflammation, ocular surface inflammation, and retinal conditions such as cystoid macular edema and VEGF-driven maculopathies.

"Eye disease" is a very broad label. The strongest evidence supports specific sub-indications (post-cataract inflammation and pain, prevention of macular edema). Each sub-indication should be assessed separately. The dry eye literature is mixed, and corneal toxicity (corneal melting) is a documented safety concern.

## Safety Considerations

- **Knowledge-graph contraindication signals** (from drug-disease links, not label text): rheumatoid arthritis, diabetes mellitus, diabetic ketoacidosis.
- **Drug interactions**: 132 interactions were found. Most of those listed are moderate, including corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, dexamethasone, triamcinolone, betamethasone, budesonide), aspirin, metformin, glimepiride, chlorpropamide, mesalazine and vancomycin. Cimetidine and linaclotide are minor.
- **Ocular surface toxicity**: the literature reports corneal melting and perforation after topical bromfenac, particularly in patients with Stevens-Johnson syndrome, Sjögren's syndrome or tear deficiency.
- **Skin reactions**: a case of toxic epidermal necrolysis after bromfenac eye drops has been reported.
- **Systemic hepatotoxicity**: oral bromfenac was withdrawn from the market because of severe liver injury. This does not directly apply to the topical form, but it is part of the drug's history.

Please also refer to the package insert for full safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Several completed Phase 3 trials (cataract surgery inflammation, dry eye disease, allergic conjunctivitis, macular edema prevention), supported by meta-analyses, support the use of bromfenac in ocular inflammatory conditions, and it is already marketed in India. However, "eye disease" is too broad to be a single indication. The ocular surface toxicity signals also call for a cautious, sub-indication-specific approach.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Selection of a specific sub-indication (for example, prevention of macular edema, or dry eye) with a dedicated evidence review
- The package insert warnings and contraindications for the Indian products, which are not yet available
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- A safety plan for patients at risk of corneal toxicity, including those with dry eye, Sjögren's or Stevens-Johnson syndrome, and for diabetic patients

**Other predicted indications:** The other nine predictions (optic papillitis, lagophthalmos, conjunctival degeneration, and others) have little or no supporting evidence and no plausible mechanistic link. They are not recommended for advancement (Hold).
