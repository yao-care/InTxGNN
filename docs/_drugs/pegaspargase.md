# Pegaspargase: From Acute Lymphoblastic Leukemia to Precursor Lymphoblastic Lymphoma/Leukemia

## One-Sentence Summary

Pegaspargase is a PEGylated L-asparaginase originally used to treat acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL).
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **precursor lymphoblastic lymphoma/leukemia**, which is essentially the same disease under a different name, so this is an established on-label use rather than a true repurposing. The direction is supported by **more than 30 registered clinical trials** and **20 publications**, including multiple completed Phase 3 trials of regimens that contain pegaspargase.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the compiled data. From known pharmacology, pegaspargase is a PEGylated form of E. coli-derived L-asparaginase. It depletes circulating asparagine. Lymphoblasts have low asparagine synthetase expression and depend on external asparagine, so depletion selectively starves them.

The predicted indication is the labeled indication (ALL) under another name, so the mechanism applies directly. Pegaspargase is a standard component of multi-agent ALL and lymphoblastic lymphoma regimens, including pediatric-inspired adult protocols.

## Safety Considerations

- **Known toxicities (from the literature):** hypersensitivity reactions, pancreatitis, hepatotoxicity, thrombosis, hypertriglyceridemia and hyperglycemia. Risk appears to rise with age and obesity in adults, and dose capping and levocarnitine have been studied to reduce hepatotoxicity.
- **Drug Interactions:** 378 interactions were identified. All 20 listed in the data are Moderate. They include:
  - corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, dexamethasone, betamethasone, triamcinolone, budesonide)
  - antidiabetic agents (metformin, acarbose, alogliptin, albiglutide, canagliflozin, dapagliflozin, saxagliptin, dulaglutide, chlorpropamide)
  - antibiotics (doxycycline, tetracycline, clarithromycin)
  - others (bupropion, acetylsalicylic acid, chenodeoxycholic acid)

  The glucose-lowering and corticosteroid interactions are relevant because pegaspargase and steroids can both cause hyperglycemia.

Label warnings and contraindications are not available in the compiled data. Please refer to the package insert for full safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
The predicted indication is the established on-label use (ALL and lymphoblastic lymphoma), supported by several completed Phase 3 trials and RCT-level literature. Pegaspargase is part of standard regimens, but most trials do not isolate its individual effect, and its toxicity profile requires active management.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the Indian registrations (currently missing, which blocks formal safety screening)
- Approved indication text for the two Indian products
- Detailed mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- A toxicity monitoring plan covering hypersensitivity, pancreatitis, hepatotoxicity, thrombosis and metabolic effects, with age and obesity considerations for adults

**Other predicted indications (not recommended at this time):**
- **Lymphoid neoplasm** (Proceed with Guardrails): the evidence comes almost entirely from ALL.
- **Hodgkin lymphoma** (Hold): no direct evidence. The literature relates to extranodal NK/T-cell lymphoma, which is a separate disease with a plausible asparaginase rationale but needs its own evaluation.
- **CLL/SLL subtypes, follicular lymphoma, blast-phase CML, methylcobalamin deficiency cblE** (Hold): prediction only or weak evidence, with no supporting mechanism or data.
