## Daclatasvir: From Chronic Hepatitis C Virus Infection to Hepatitis B Virus Infection
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Daclatasvir is an NS5A inhibitor originally used to treat chronic hepatitis C virus (HCV) infection.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Hepatitis B Virus Infection**, but the supporting evidence is weak: the **4 clinical trials** and **20 publications** retrieved all concern HCV treatment (including in HBV co-infected patients) and show no anti-HBV efficacy. Several reports describe HBV reactivation during daclatasvir-based therapy.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, daclatasvir is a direct-acting antiviral that targets the HCV NS5A protein, part of the replication complex. It is used with other agents such as sofosbuvir for HCV, and its efficacy there is well established.

The link to HBV is weak. HBV and HCV are both hepatotropic viruses. But HBV is a DNA virus that replicates through reverse transcription, and it has no NS5A equivalent. Daclatasvir has no established anti-HBV activity. The very high score comes from the TxGNN model (deep learning) and may reflect similarities the model learned between related diseases, not biological evidence.

The clinical literature points the other way. Case reports and cohorts describe HBV reactivation or acute hepatitis B in HBV/HCV co-infected patients during daclatasvir-based therapy. This is a safety signal, not a therapeutic one.

The other top predictions (HIV, hepatitis E, hepatitis A, Omsk hemorrhagic fever, Kyasanur forest disease, and animal or neurodevelopmental conditions) rest on even less evidence. Their scores come from the TxGNN model (deep learning) and may reflect similarities the model learned between related diseases, not biological evidence. They are not pursued here.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: 89 interactions are recorded. Examples of major-level interactions include dexamethasone, clarithromycin, eluxadoline and cobicistat. Moderate-level examples include metformin, loperamide, aprepitant, rosuvastatin, simvastatin and apixaban.
- **HBV reactivation**: The literature describes HBV reactivation and acute hepatitis B in HBV/HCV co-infected patients during daclatasvir-based therapy. HBV screening and monitoring are important in this setting.

Please refer to the package insert for warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Daclatasvir has no established anti-HBV mechanism. The available trials and publications all concern HCV treatment, and the HBV-related reports raise a safety concern (reactivation) rather than showing efficacy. The high TxGNN score likely reflects network proximity between viral hepatitis nodes.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- In vitro evidence of anti-HBV activity (for example, HBV replication assays) before any clinical consideration
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the India regulatory source
- A clear HBV reactivation risk-management plan for any co-infected population
