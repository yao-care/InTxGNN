## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Haloperidol is generally understood as a high-affinity dopamine D2 receptor antagonist, and its efficacy in psychotic disorders is well established.

No plausible mechanistic link could be identified between D2 antagonism and defective fucosylation, a congenital metabolic disorder of protein glycosylation. The high score (99.91%) is a model output only. Haloperidol could at most offer symptomatic help for neuropsychiatric features, which would not be disease-modifying. This prediction should therefore be treated as a hypothesis with no supporting evidence.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction has only a model score, with no clinical trials, no literature and no plausible mechanistic link to haloperidol's D2 antagonism. The patient group is also vulnerable, and haloperidol carries QT, extrapyramidal and haematological risks, so there is no basis to proceed.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanistic or preclinical evidence linking haloperidol to the fucosylation pathway
- Detailed mechanism of action data (MOA)
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the Indian regulator
- A safety plan covering QT prolongation, extrapyramidal symptoms and the high interaction burden

**Note on other predictions:** Among the other predictions compiled for haloperidol, **manic bipolar affective disorder** has far stronger support. It has completed Phase 3 trials (for example NCT00129220, a haloperidol-controlled olanzapine trial) and network meta-analyses (2022, 2025), and carries an L1 rating and a "Proceed with Guardrails" recommendation. It is closer to established clinical use than to novel repurposing. It would be a more productive candidate for evaluation than the prediction in this report.
