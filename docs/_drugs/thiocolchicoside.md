## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in this Evidence Pack. Based on known pharmacological information, Thiocolchicoside belongs to the muscle relaxant drug class, acting on GABA-A and glycine receptors in the central nervous system to reduce muscle hypertonia and spasm. Its efficacy in musculoskeletal spasm has been established through long-standing clinical use in multiple markets.

Mechanistically, GABA-A and glycine receptor modulation is also implicated in sleep regulation and central nervous system inhibition, which could theoretically provide a plausible — but currently unproven — link between Thiocolchicoside's known muscle-relaxant/sedative properties and a potential effect on insomnia. However, this link has not yet been substantiated by disease-specific clinical or literature evidence in the data reviewed.

The one clinical trial retrieved for this prediction (NCT06791434) is a Phase 4 study of dry needling as an add-on therapy for myofascial low back pain — it does not investigate insomnia and does not mention Thiocolchicoside directly relevant outcomes for sleep. This suggests the current evidence base for the insomnia prediction is essentially model-driven rather than clinically validated.
