# Captopril: From Hypertension to Malignant Renovascular Hypertension

## One-Sentence Summary

Captopril is an ACE inhibitor originally used to treat hypertension and diabetic nephropathy.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **malignant renovascular hypertension**,
but **no clinical trials** are registered, and the **19 publications** retrieved are mostly reviews, case reports and diagnostic studies rather than therapeutic evidence.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, captopril is an ACE inhibitor that blocks the formation of angiotensin II. Its efficacy in hypertension is established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to malignant renovascular hypertension.

Renovascular hypertension, particularly the malignant form, is driven by activation of the renin-angiotensin system (RAS) due to reduced blood flow to the kidney. Blocking this pathway is therefore mechanistically coherent. The predicted indication is essentially a severe subtype of the already approved hypertension indication.

An important caution: in bilateral renal artery stenosis or a solitary kidney, ACE inhibition can precipitate acute renal failure. This directly affects the patients targeted by this prediction.

Other predictions from the model (malignant hypertensive renal disease, and two forms of pulmonary hypertension) have little or no supporting evidence. They are speculative and are not pursued here. Systemic ACE inhibition is not established therapy for pulmonary hypertension, and systemic hypotension is a risk.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications / risk conditions (from the knowledge graph, not label text):** kidney disease, liver disease, pancreatitis, gallbladder disease, angioedema (including hereditary, acquired and RAAS-blocker-induced forms), systemic lupus erythematosus, diabetes mellitus and diabetic ketoacidosis, potassium deficiency, neutropenia and agranulocytosis, aortic stenosis and aortic valve disease, hypotensive disorder, hyperparathyroidism, hepatic coma and encephalopathy, connective tissue disease, and bone disease.
- **Renal caution specific to this indication:** ACE inhibition in bilateral renal artery stenosis or a solitary kidney can precipitate acute renal failure.
- **Drug interactions:** 245 interactions are recorded. Those listed include moderate interactions with corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, dexamethasone, betamethasone, triamcinolone, budesonide), antidiabetic drugs (metformin, alogliptin, canagliflozin, dapagliflozin, empagliflozin, ertugliflozin, exenatide, chlorpropamide, acetohexamide), as well as bupropion, morphine, acetylsalicylic acid and dronabinol. Minor interactions include aluminium hydroxide and calcium carbonate.

Package insert warnings and contraindications were not available; please refer to the package insert for authoritative safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The mechanistic rationale is sound and captopril is already marketed for hypertension in India. However, there are no registered clinical trials, and the retrieved literature is largely diagnostic or descriptive rather than showing therapeutic benefit in malignant renovascular hypertension. Renal risk in the target population is also a concern.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the Indian regulatory sources, which are required for safety screening
- Detailed mechanism of action data (e.g., from DrugBank)
- Therapeutic clinical evidence (trials or well-designed observational studies) of captopril in malignant or renovascular hypertension
- A renal safety plan for patients with bilateral renal artery stenosis or a solitary kidney, including monitoring of renal function and potassium
