## Nizatidine: From Gastric Ulcer to Active Peptic Ulcer Disease
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Nizatidine is an H2-receptor antagonist originally used to treat gastric ulcer, duodenal ulcer, gastroesophageal reflux disease and related acid-related conditions.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **active peptic ulcer disease**, but this is essentially an on-label or near-label use rather than true repurposing.
Support comes from **0 registered clinical trials** and **19 publications**, mostly older clinical studies and reviews on duodenal ulcer.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, nizatidine belongs to the H2-receptor antagonist class. It suppresses basal, nocturnal and stimulated gastric acid secretion, as described in the literature (for example the 1988 review in *Drugs*).

Active peptic ulcer disease is closely related to nizatidine's original indications (gastric and duodenal ulcer). Acid suppression is the mechanism behind both, so the prediction is mechanistically reasonable. It is better read as confirmation of an existing use than as a new therapeutic direction.

Other predicted indications are weaker:
- **Gastrojejunal ulcer** and **gastroduodenitis**: acid-related and plausible, but supported only by indirect literature (duodenal ulcer, NSAID-induced injury).
- **Peptic ulcer perforation**, **duodenal obstruction**, and **multiple endocrine neoplasia**: prediction only, with no trials or literature. These are surgical complications or conditions where PPIs are the standard acid-suppression therapy.
- **Duodenogastric reflux**: the link to H2-receptor blockade is weak, because the pathology is driven by bile and pancreatic secretions rather than mainly by acid.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications (from the knowledge graph, not label text)**: liver disease, gallbladder disease, impaired renal function, and gastric cancer or neoplasm (including gastric carcinoma, diffuse gastric adenocarcinoma and linitis plastica).
- **Drug Interactions**: 263 interactions are recorded in total. Examples include:
  - **Major**: atazanavir, dasatinib
  - **Moderate**: acalabrutinib, bosutinib, brigatinib, cefditoren, cefpodoxime, cefuroxime, ceritinib, chlorpropamide, dabrafenib, dacomitinib
  - **Minor**: ketorolac, ibuprofen, alendronic acid, axitinib, cyanocobalamin, diclofenac, magnesium oxide, oxaprozin

  Several of these (atazanavir, dasatinib and the other kinase inhibitors) are consistent with reduced absorption when gastric acid is suppressed.

Package insert warnings and contraindications for the India product were not available. Please refer to the package insert for complete safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The evidence level is L4: there are no registered trials, and the literature consists mainly of older duodenal ulcer studies and reviews, with study designs not fully verifiable from the available titles. Because this is essentially an existing use, it adds little repurposing value. The other predicted indications rely on indirect or no evidence.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- India package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Verification of the design and phase of the key duodenal ulcer studies
- Approved indication text for the India license, to confirm whether peptic ulcer disease is already covered
- Evaluation of the interaction burden with drugs that depend on gastric acidity for absorption
