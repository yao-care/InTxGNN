## Pimozide: From Tourette Syndrome to Trichotillomania
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Pimozide is a dopamine D2 antagonist antipsychotic, originally used to treat Tourette syndrome.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **trichotillomania** (compulsive hair pulling).
Support is limited: **no registered clinical trials** and **9 publications**, mostly reviews, plus one small, dated augmentation study from 1992.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Pimozide is known to be a dopamine D2 receptor antagonist, and its efficacy in Tourette syndrome is established.

Trichotillomania and Tourette syndrome share features of repetitive, habit-like behaviour. Both are thought to involve dopaminergic dysregulation in cortico-striatal-thalamic circuits. A 1992 study (PMID 1532960) reported that low-dose pimozide added to serotonin reuptake inhibitors was helpful in trichotillomania. A dermatology review (PMID 15554735) also covers pimozide use in related psychodermatologic conditions.

However, the evidence is small-scale and dated. More recent evidence maps favour other approaches, such as SSRIs, N-acetylcysteine, olanzapine and behavioural therapy.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications (knowledge graph associations, not label text)**: long QT syndrome (including familial), torsades de pointes, sudden cardiac arrest, heart conduction disease, epilepsy, kidney disease, liver disease, gallbladder disease, potassium deficiency, benign prostatic hyperplasia, glaucoma, neuroleptic malignant syndrome, neutropenia, agranulocytosis, leukopenia and related congenital neutropenias, female breast carcinoma, and Alzheimer disease.
- **Drug Interactions**: 247 interactions were recorded. Major ones include hydrocortisone, amphotericin B (including the lipid complex), bupropion, aprepitant, morphine, lorcaserin, cimetidine, potassium citrate, clarithromycin, picosulfuric acid, and polyethylene glycol with electrolytes. Moderate ones include famotidine, epinephrine, hyoscyamine, loperamide, atropine, bisacodyl, glycopyrronium, and dicyclomine.

Please also refer to the package insert for label-based warnings and contraindications, which were not available for this report.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Support for trichotillomania is limited to a small 1992 augmentation study and reviews, with no registered trials. Pimozide also carries QT-prolongation risk and a heavy interaction burden, while better-supported alternatives exist.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications for the Indian-marketed products, which are required before safety screening
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Modern controlled trial data for pimozide in trichotillomania, with a comparison against current options (SSRIs, N-acetylcysteine, olanzapine, behavioural therapy)
- A cardiac (QT/ECG) monitoring and drug interaction management plan
