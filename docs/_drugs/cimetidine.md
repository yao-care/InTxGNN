## Cimetidine: Histamine H2-receptor antagonist → peptic ulcer perforation
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Cimetidine is a histamine H2-receptor antagonist that reduces stomach acid. The TxGNN model predicts it may be relevant for **peptic ulcer perforation**, but no clinical trials are registered and the 20 publications compiled for this report are mostly case reports, reviews and observational analyses. Several of them describe perforation occurring while patients were taking cimetidine.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Cimetidine belongs to the H2-receptor antagonist class, which lowers gastric acid secretion. In principle, acid suppression could support healing of the ulcer that underlies a perforation.

However, perforation is an acute surgical emergency, and acid suppression is not a treatment for it. Several reports in the literature describe perforation occurring while patients were already on cimetidine (PMIDs 3967139, 6743974, 7082957). This argues against efficacy in preventing or treating this complication. The high model score most likely reflects the general association between cimetidine and peptic ulcer disease rather than a true therapeutic signal.

No cited study tests cimetidine as a treatment for peptic ulcer perforation. The evidence level is therefore L5.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
No clinical trial or study directly supports cimetidine for peptic ulcer perforation. Case reports show perforation occurring during cimetidine therapy, so the prediction looks like an artifact of the drug's link to ulcer disease.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Direct clinical evidence in perforated ulcer, such as controlled studies of post-operative or conservative management
- Mechanism of action data
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the Indian regulatory source
- A review of the interaction profile for likely co-medications
