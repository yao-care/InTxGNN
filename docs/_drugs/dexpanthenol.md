## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, dexpanthenol is converted in the body to pantothenic acid, a precursor of coenzyme A. This pathway supports epithelial repair and wound healing, and dexpanthenol has also been used as a stimulant of gastrointestinal motility.

The original indications span skin disease and gut motility disorders, so a link to anorectal conditions is plausible. Mucosal healing and bowel function are both relevant to the anorectal region.

However, anorectal stricture is a fibrotic, structural problem. No data show that dexpanthenol benefits this type of condition, so the prediction remains unproven.

Other predictions with the same score range (all L5, Hold unless noted):
- **Imperforate anus**: a congenital malformation that needs surgery. Dexpanthenol could at most support postoperative skin care.
- **Anal polyp**: no clear mechanistic rationale. The prediction likely reflects network proximity to related indications.
- **Vulvar inverted follicular keratosis**: an indirect link through keratotic skin conditions. The lesion is usually excised.
- **Proctitis**: a plausible link through mucosal regeneration and anti-inflammatory effects, but unproven.
- **Punctate epithelial keratoconjunctivitis**: a plausible link through corneal epithelial healing, which fits the ophthalmic gels marketed in India. No supporting trials were provided.
- **Exanthem**: the only prediction with a trial (see below). It is rated L4 with a "Research Question" recommendation.
