## One-Sentence Summary

Bupropion is an antidepressant that inhibits the reuptake of norepinephrine and dopamine. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **attention deficit-hyperactivity disorder**, and the data compiled for this report include **6 clinical trials** (4 of them completed with a direct ADHD focus) and **20 publications**, including a Cochrane review of bupropion in adult ADHD.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data are not available in the compiled data. From the literature, bupropion's main action is believed to be inhibition of dopamine and norepinephrine reuptake, and it has no serotonergic activity.

Established ADHD treatments, such as stimulants and atomoxetine, act on these same catecholamine pathways. Reduced dopamine and norepinephrine signalling is thought to underlie the inattention and hyperactivity of ADHD, so a drug that raises synaptic levels of both is mechanistically plausible. Several reviews in the evidence list bupropion among the non-stimulant options for adults who do not respond to, or cannot tolerate, stimulants.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: 665 interactions are recorded. Major-level interactions include fentanyl, lidocaine, codeine, dihydrocodeine, tramadol, methylphenidate, hydrocortisone, aldesleukin, alfentanil and amantadine. Moderate-level interactions include ethanol, nifedipine, alprazolam, cetirizine and hydrochlorothiazide. Bupropion inhibits CYP2D6, which contributes to the interaction burden.
- **Conditions flagged for caution or contraindication in the compiled data**: epilepsy, hypertension, liver disease and cirrhosis, kidney disease, bipolar mania, psychotic disorders, anorexia and bulimia nervosa, substance dependence (including opiate dependence), recent myocardial infarction, and central nervous system neoplasm.
- **Other points to watch**: seizure risk is dose-dependent, blood pressure should be monitored, and caution is needed in patients with substance use comorbidity.

Package insert warnings and contraindications were not available; please refer to the package insert for full safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
A completed Phase 3 placebo-controlled trial, a Phase 2/3 trial and a Phase 4 trial directly study bupropion in ADHD, and a Cochrane review addresses its use in adults. The Phase 3 trial's comparator and arms should be checked, and the seizure, blood pressure and interaction risks call for guardrails.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the local regulator
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Verification of the Phase 3 trial's design, results and dose arms
- A monitoring plan for seizure risk, blood pressure and substance use comorbidity
- A medication review to manage the large number of drug interactions, especially CYP2D6-metabolized drugs
