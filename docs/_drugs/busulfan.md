## Busulfan: Alkylating agent for conditioning before transplant → myelodysplastic syndrome
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Busulfan is a bifunctional alkylating agent that cross-links DNA and ablates bone marrow, and it is used mainly as a conditioning drug before stem cell transplant.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **myelodysplastic syndrome**, with **39 listed clinical trials** and **19 publications** in the data compiled for this report.
Its role would be as a conditioning component within an allogeneic transplant strategy, not as a stand-alone MDS therapy.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the compiled data. Based on known information, busulfan is an alkylating agent that cross-links DNA and causes myeloablation.

Allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation is the only potentially curative approach for eligible patients with higher-risk MDS. Busulfan-based conditioning clears the malignant clone and makes room for donor stem cells to engraft. This is the mechanistic link between busulfan and MDS.

The supporting studies test busulfan as part of multi-drug regimens (with fludarabine, cyclophosphamide, cladribine, venetoclax or decitabine). The evidence therefore supports busulfan as a transplant conditioning backbone, not as a single agent for MDS.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: 228 interactions were recorded. Examples include metronidazole (Major), aprepitant (Moderate) and miconazole (Moderate), and levofloxacin (Minor). Many others, such as dexamethasone, ondansetron, omeprazole and morphine, have an unclassified severity level.
- Conditions flagged in the compiled data as concerns for busulfan include neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, agranulocytosis, anemia, pulmonary fibrosis, epilepsy, and infectious diseases. These are not taken from label text and need confirmation against the package insert.
- A literature review notes that secondary malignancy risk with busulfan exposure is considered low but poorly characterized (PMID 37856098).

Please refer to the package insert for full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Two completed Phase 3 randomized trials (NCT00005866, NCT00822393) and several randomized publications directly address busulfan-based conditioning in MDS. The evidence supports busulfan only as part of transplant conditioning regimens in selected patients, not as a stand-alone MDS treatment.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications for the Indian-marketed products
- Mechanism of action data to complete the mechanistic analysis
- Clear restriction of use to transplant-conditioning settings in transplant-eligible patients
- A myelosuppression and toxicity monitoring plan, including dose targeting and supportive care
