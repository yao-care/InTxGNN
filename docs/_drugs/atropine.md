# Atropine: From Anticholinergic Use (Original Indication Not Specified in Licence Data) to Migraine Disorder

## One-Sentence Summary

Atropine is a non-selective muscarinic antagonist marketed in India in injectable, oral and eye-drop products, though the licence records compiled for this report do not state an approved indication.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **migraine disorder**, but there are **0 clinical trials** and only **indirect, mostly preclinical literature**, so this is a research question rather than a supported use.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on general pharmacology, atropine is a non-selective muscarinic acetylcholine receptor antagonist, and its effect on migraine would therefore have to come from blocking cholinergic signalling.

The literature gives a plausible but indirect link:
- Meningeal mast cell-mediated cholinergic modulation of neurogenic inflammation in a rat migraine model (PMID 36485173).
- Parasympathetic (sphenopalatine ganglion) stimulation causing dural plasma protein extravasation in rats (PMID 9344563).
- Autonomic involvement in chronic paroxysmal hemicrania, where systemic atropine reduced attack-related sweating, tearing and nasal secretion (PMID 2943405).

All of this is preclinical or physiological. No study tests atropine as a migraine treatment in humans. Several retrieved papers are off-topic or about adverse events (topiramate, botulinum toxin, a perioperative case report, ergot pharmacology) and add no support.

A second predicted indication, **migraine with brainstem aura** (score 99.42%, evidence level L5), rests on a single mouse study (PMID 31945385). That study shows cholinergic *activation* inhibits cortical spreading depression, which, if anything, argues against an antagonist such as atropine. It is not recommended for pursuit.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: 380 interactions were recorded. Examples at moderate level include botulinum toxin type A, fentanyl, chlorpheniramine, acebutolol, codeine, tramadol, amitriptyline, amantadine, galantamine, loperamide, hyoscyamine and ethanol. Acetaminophen and hydrochlorothiazide are listed as minor.
- **Expected class effects**: Peripheral anticholinergic effects (dry mouth, blurred vision, tachycardia, urinary retention, cognitive effects) are a concern for chronic migraine prophylaxis. This comes from general pharmacology, not from label data compiled here.

Please refer to the package insert for warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The TxGNN score is very high (99.56%), but the supporting evidence is limited to preclinical and indirect studies, with no registered trials and no human data on atropine for migraine. The anticholinergic adverse-effect profile and the large interaction burden further limit feasibility for chronic use.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the Indian licences (currently a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Approved indication text for the existing licences
- Preclinical or early human proof-of-concept data for atropine in migraine
- A feasibility assessment of route and dose (e.g. non-systemic delivery) to limit anticholinergic adverse effects
