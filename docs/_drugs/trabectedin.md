## Trabectedin: From Ovarian Cancer to Female Breast Carcinoma
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Trabectedin is a marine-derived DNA-binding cytotoxic drug, originally used to treat ovarian cancer (and, in Europe, soft tissue sarcoma).
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Female Breast Carcinoma**,
with **1 clinical trial** (a safety-focused QT study) and **20 publications** currently related to this direction. Published single-agent Phase 2 breast cancer data show only modest activity.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Trabectedin binds the minor groove of DNA and causes DNA damage. It interferes with transcription and DNA-repair machinery. It is particularly active in cells with deficient homologous recombination repair, such as BRCA1/2-deficient tumours. Formal mechanism-of-action data were not available in the data compiled for this report, so this description draws on the published literature.

Ovarian and breast cancers share DNA-repair biology, especially in BRCA-associated disease and triple-negative breast cancer. Trabectedin also reduces tumour-associated macrophages and modulates the tumour microenvironment. Preclinical work suggests it enhances the antitumour effect of IL-12 in triple-negative breast cancer.

The high TxGNN score is a model prediction, not clinical proof. Published single-agent Phase 2 data in anthracycline- and taxane-pretreated advanced breast cancer show only modest activity, so efficacy in breast cancer remains unestablished.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: 450 interactions were identified. Among those listed, **clarithromycin** is rated Major. Moderate interactions include aprepitant, dexamethasone, cimetidine, metronidazole, miconazole, levofloxacin, minocycline, bupropion, naltrexone, rosuvastatin and simvastatin. Several others are also listed, including antidiabetic agents (acarbose, pioglitazone, rosiglitazone). The full list should be reviewed before any clinical use.

Please refer to the package insert for other safety information (warnings and contraindications).

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The mechanism is plausible, especially for BRCA-deficient and triple-negative breast cancer, and the TxGNN score is high. However, the only registered trial is a QT safety study, and published single-agent Phase 2 data show modest activity. There is no completed Phase 3 evidence in breast cancer. This is best treated as a research question rather than a candidate for advancing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the India registration (currently a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Approved indication text for the Indian registration
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank
- Full review of the Phase 2 breast cancer results (PMIDs 25239225, 24692579, 27266804), focusing on BRCA-mutated and triple-negative subgroups
- A biomarker-driven trial design (e.g., BRCA or XPG status) if development continues
- Review of the clarithromycin and other drug interactions for the proposed patient population
