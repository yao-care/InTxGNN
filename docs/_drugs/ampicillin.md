## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Ampicillin is a beta-lactam antibiotic that blocks bacterial cell wall synthesis. Detailed mechanism of action data are not otherwise available in the data compiled for this report, so this description rests on the drug's known class behaviour.

Some bacterial infections of the upper airway, such as epiglottitis, laryngeal abscess and deep neck infection, overlap biologically with laryngitis. Ampicillin has been used in several of these conditions, for example in paediatric epiglottitis, so a bacterial-airway link is plausible.

There are important limits. Most laryngitis is viral, so an antibacterial would not be expected to help in most cases. The retrieved papers mostly concern epiglottitis, abscess, croup or neck infections rather than laryngitis itself. They contain no direct efficacy data for ampicillin in laryngitis. Under the evidence rules, this places the prediction at L5: model prediction only, with no cited study that directly addresses the disease.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction score is very high, but there are no clinical trials and no publication directly studies ampicillin in laryngitis. Most laryngitis is viral, so a benefit is doubtful, and antibiotic stewardship also argues against use without stronger evidence.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Controlled studies of ampicillin in bacterially confirmed laryngitis
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the local licences
