# Capecitabine: From Gastric Cancer to Gastric Tubular Adenocarcinoma

## One-Sentence Summary

Capecitabine is an oral fluoropyrimidine chemotherapy originally used for gastric cancer and colon cancer metastases.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **gastric tubular adenocarcinoma** (top-ranked candidate with supporting evidence). The literature supports this direction with **20 publications**, including multiple Phase 3 RCTs, but no registered clinical trials were retrieved for this specific term. Because tubular adenocarcinoma is a common histologic subtype of gastric adenocarcinoma, this is essentially an existing indication restated, not true repurposing.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, capecitabine is an oral prodrug converted in the body to 5-fluorouracil (5-FU), which inhibits thymidylate synthase and damages RNA and DNA. This antimetabolite activity is the basis of its use in gastrointestinal cancers.

Tubular adenocarcinoma is one of the most common histologic patterns of gastric adenocarcinoma, which is already a listed original indication. The prediction is therefore mechanistically straightforward: the same drug acting through the same pathway on the same disease family.

Other predicted indications in the data follow a similar logic, mostly anatomic or histologic subtypes of gastric cancer (cardia, body, pylorus, signet ring cell, EBV-associated). Their evidence varies widely. Some, such as gastric cardia adenocarcinoma and gastric body carcinoma, have completed trials, while others, such as malignant gastric granular cell tumor, have only a model score and a weak biological rationale.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications / risk conditions (from knowledge graph, not label text)**: liver disease, gallbladder disease, thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, agranulocytosis, anemia (including macrocytic anemia), hyperbilirubinemia (including hereditary forms and Gilbert syndrome), impaired renal function, coronary artery disease, sudden cardiac arrest, heart conduction disease, infectious disease, necrotizing enterocolitis, stomatitis, and **dihydropyrimidine dehydrogenase (DPD) deficiency**.
- **Drug Interactions**: 335 interactions were identified in total. Metronomidazole-class agents show moderate interactions: **metronidazole** and **tinidazole**. Minor interactions include levofloxacin and antacids (aluminum hydroxide, magaldrate, magnesium carbonate, hydroxide and oxide). Several others (e.g., pantoprazole, omeprazole, lansoprazole, morphine, metformin, simvastatin) have an unclassified severity level.

Package insert warnings and contraindications for the India products were not available and should be reviewed.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Gastric tubular adenocarcinoma is a histologic subtype of gastric adenocarcinoma, an existing indication, and multiple Phase 3 RCTs of capecitabine-containing regimens (e.g., CLASSIC, RESOLVE) support use in the parent disease. This is closer to on-label use than true repurposing, but the retrieved records are not specific to the tubular subtype, and several trials are not capecitabine-specific.

**Other predicted indications in brief:**
- Gastric cardia adenocarcinoma: Proceed with Guardrails (two completed Phase 2 trials, L2).
- Gastric body carcinoma: Proceed with Guardrails (completed Phase 3, but capecitabine's role unconfirmed).
- Signet ring cell, EBV-associated: Research Question (indirect or immunotherapy-driven evidence).
- Gastric adenocarcinoma with proximal polyposis, microinvasive gastric cancer, gastric pylorus carcinoma, malignant gastric granular cell tumor: Hold (prediction only or minimal evidence).
- Carcinoma of stomach, salivary gland type: Research Question (only generic Phase 1 solid-tumor studies).

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications for the India-marketed products (a blocking data gap for safety screening)
- Detailed mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Trial-by-trial confirmation of capecitabine's contribution in the cited combination studies
- DPD deficiency screening and a safety monitoring plan for patients with liver, renal, cardiac or hematologic risk conditions
- Review of interactions with metronidazole and tinidazole in clinical use
