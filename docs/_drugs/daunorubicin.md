# Daunorubicin: From Acute Leukemia to Hodgkin's Lymphoma

## One-Sentence Summary

Daunorubicin is an anthracycline chemotherapy drug, originally used to treat acute lymphoblastic and acute myeloid leukemia.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Hodgkin's lymphoma**,
but the supporting evidence is indirect: **2 Phase 2 clinical trials** (both of multi-drug regimens, one in non-Hodgkin lymphoma) and a set of mostly review-type or non-Hodgkin lymphoma publications.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, daunorubicin is an anthracycline that intercalates into DNA and inhibits topoisomerase II. Its efficacy in acute leukemia is well established.

Doxorubicin, a closely related anthracycline with the same mechanism, is a backbone of Hodgkin lymphoma regimens such as ABVD. This makes it mechanistically plausible that daunorubicin could be active in Hodgkin lymphoma. Review literature also notes that anthracyclines as a class form the backbone of regimens for leukemia, Hodgkin lymphoma and several paediatric solid tumours.

However, the available trials are multi-drug studies, so daunorubicin's own contribution cannot be separated from the other agents. The prediction should be read as a research question, not as proof of benefit.

- **Drug Interactions**: 387 interactions were identified. Major-level examples include cisapride, dolasetron, papaverine, deferiprone and macimorelin. Moderate-level examples include famotidine, loperamide, clarithromycin, levofloxacin, ondansetron, granisetron and palonosetron.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The mechanistic rationale is plausible, and doxorubicin is already part of standard Hodgkin lymphoma regimens. However, the only trials are Phase 2 multi-drug studies, one of which is in non-Hodgkin lymphoma, and none isolates daunorubicin's contribution in Hodgkin lymphoma. Evidence is limited to L3, and the safety profile (myelosuppression, cardiotoxicity) is substantial. Other predicted indications (for example neuroblastoma and ALK-positive large B-cell lymphoma) have similarly indirect evidence, and several have no evidence beyond the prediction score.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings, contraindications and approved indications from the India registrations
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Trials or studies testing daunorubicin specifically in Hodgkin lymphoma, with the drug composition of the multi-agent regimens confirmed
- A cardiac and haematological safety monitoring plan, including cumulative anthracycline dose limits
