# Procarbazine: From Hodgkin Lymphoma to Follicular Lymphoma

## One-Sentence Summary

Procarbazine is an oral alkylating-type chemotherapy agent, originally used to treat Hodgkin lymphoma.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **follicular lymphoma**,
but the supporting evidence is limited: **2 related clinical trials** (neither directly demonstrating procarbazine efficacy) and **12 publications**, mostly small series, reviews and older cohorts.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, procarbazine is an oral alkylating-type agent already used in lymphoma regimens such as those for Hodgkin lymphoma. Its efficacy in that setting is established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to other B-cell lymphomas such as follicular lymphoma.

The literature offers some support. A review titled "Procarbazine for non-Hodgkin's lymphoma" describes two patients with relapsed or refractory follicular lymphoma who achieved a complete and durable remission with prolonged daily procarbazine. The PEP-C metronomic oral regimen (prednisone, etoposide, procarbazine, cyclophosphamide) has also been studied in relapsed or refractory lymphoma.

However, modern follicular lymphoma care relies on anti-CD20 antibody-based regimens. Procarbazine's role today is unclear, and the evidence is limited to case-level reports and small studies.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindication-related disease associations (knowledge graph, not label text)**: liver failure, liver disease, kidney disease, gallbladder disease, thrombocytopenia, leukopenia, anaemia (including macrocytic anaemia), blood coagulation disease, peripheral neuropathy and other peripheral nervous system disease, infectious disease (including fungal infection and dermatophytosis), pyoureter, and substance abuse/dependence.
- **Drug Interactions**: 433 interactions recorded. Major interactions in the data include isometheptene, bupropion, morphine, lorcaserin, diethylpropion, phentermine, fenfluramine, dexfenfluramine, mazindol, ephedrine, and the 5-HT3 antagonists dolasetron, palonosetron, ondansetron and granisetron. Moderate interactions include epinephrine (various forms) and metoclopramide. Levofloxacin is a minor interaction.
- **Long-term risks**: The literature shows procarbazine carcinogenicity in animal bioassays, and gonadal toxicity (including azoospermia) in survivors of procarbazine-containing regimens.

Package insert warnings and contraindications were not available; please refer to the package insert for label safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold (research question)**

**Rationale:**
Evidence for follicular lymphoma is limited to a small case-level report, a metronomic regimen cohort, and older regimens, with no trial directly demonstrating procarbazine efficacy. Standard care now relies on anti-CD20-based regimens, and procarbazine carries significant marrow and long-term toxicity. The high TxGNN score is not yet supported by robust clinical data.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the India market labels (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data (e.g. from DrugBank)
- Verification of the regimen composition in NCT00003113
- Comparative evidence against current anti-CD20-based standards in follicular lymphoma
- Evaluation of the lower-ranked predictions (neuroblastoma, ganglioneuroblastoma, retroperitoneal neoplasm, and a rare vertebral/endocrine/T-cell syndrome), which have little or no clinical support at this stage
