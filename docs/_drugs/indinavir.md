## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, indinavir is an HIV-1 protease inhibitor. Its efficacy in HIV infection has been established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to simian immunodeficiency virus (SIV), a closely related lentivirus.

In vitro studies support this link. In one study, indinavir inhibited SIVmac239 with a 50% effective concentration of about 39 nM, compared with about 66 nM for HIV-1. Macaque studies also used indinavir as part of combination antiretroviral regimens.

However, SIV does not infect humans. The value of this prediction is as a research model for HIV, not as a new therapeutic indication for patients. Other predictions in the list are more relevant to human care but largely overlap with the original indication. These include congenital HIV (a perinatal and pediatric population) and AIDS-related complex (a historical term for symptomatic HIV disease).

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The SIV prediction rests only on in vitro and macaque studies, with no human trials. SIV does not infect humans, so this is a research-model finding rather than a viable new indication. Indinavir has been superseded by newer agents. The remaining predictions (feline AIDS, a rare neurodevelopmental disorder, obsolete familial combined hyperlipidemia, fibroma of prostate) have no supporting evidence. Their scores come from the TxGNN model (deep learning) and may reflect similarities the model learned between related diseases, not biological evidence.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the India regulatory source, which are required for safety screening
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Clarification of whether a veterinary or research-model use of indinavir is actually of interest, since no human repurposing case exists for this prediction
