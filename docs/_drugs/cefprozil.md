# Cefprozil: From Respiratory and ENT Infections to Urinary Tract Infection

## One-Sentence Summary

Cefprozil is an oral cephalosporin antibiotic, originally used for respiratory tract, ear-nose-throat and skin infections such as bronchitis, tonsillitis and otitis media.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **urinary tract infection (UTI)**,
with **3 randomized comparative trials** (all from 1991-1992) and several reviews supporting this direction. No registered clinical trials exist for this indication.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the compiled data. Based on known information, cefprozil is a beta-lactam cephalosporin that inhibits bacterial cell wall synthesis. Its efficacy in respiratory, ENT and skin infections is established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to bacterial infections of the urinary tract.

Cefprozil shows good in vitro activity against common uropathogens such as *E. coli* and *Klebsiella pneumoniae*, and because it is excreted by the kidneys it reaches high urinary concentrations. Three comparative trials against cefaclor in acute uncomplicated UTI support clinical activity.

Important caveats: the evidence is dated (1991-1992), UTI is not on the current label (approved uses are respiratory, ENT and skin infections), and cefprozil is not a first-line choice for UTI under current guidelines.

Other predictions in the list are less credible. Several (Ureaplasma urethritis, urogenital and abdominal tuberculosis) are likely false positives because the organisms are lacking a cell wall or intrinsically resistant to beta-lactams. Pneumonia and laryngitis are plausible but only weakly supported.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications (knowledge-graph associations, not label text)**: *Clostridium difficile* colitis, perinatal necrotizing enterocolitis, congenital prothrombin deficiency.
- **Drug Interactions**: 55 interactions were recorded. Those rated Moderate include balsalazide, kanamycin, neomycin, streptomycin and picosulfuric acid. Others (e.g., acetylsalicylic acid, amoxicillin, proton pump inhibitors, H2 blockers, corticosteroids, metformin, simvastatin, potassium chloride) are of unknown severity.

Please refer to the package insert for the full warnings and contraindications, which were not available in the compiled data.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Three randomized trials show cefprozil performed comparably to cefaclor in uncomplicated UTI, but the evidence is over 30 years old, no trials are registered, and UTI is outside the current label. Current guidelines do not position cefprozil as first-line for UTI. It is best treated as a research question rather than a development priority.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications (currently a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Comparison with current first-line UTI agents and contemporary local resistance data (India)
- Modern controlled evidence or a clear regulatory rationale for extending the indication
