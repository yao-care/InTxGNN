## Fluoxetine: From Depression and Anxiety Disorders to Schizoid Personality Disorder
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Fluoxetine is a selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor (SSRI) originally used for depressive, anxiety, obsessive-compulsive and eating disorders such as bulimia nervosa.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **schizoid personality disorder**,
but there are **no registered clinical trials** and only **2 publications** (a narrative review and a depression cohort study), so this prediction rests almost entirely on the model.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Fluoxetine is known as an SSRI, and its efficacy in depression, anxiety and obsessive-compulsive disorders is established. Serotonergic modulation may plausibly affect affective flattening and social withdrawal, which are features of schizoid personality disorder.

However, no specific mechanism has been established for this condition. The only support is a narrative review of drug treatment in cluster A personality disorders (paranoid, schizoid and schizotypal), which does not show that fluoxetine works in schizoid personality disorder. The model score is high, but the clinical rationale is weak.

For context, other predictions in the same group show stronger support. Social phobia (phobic disorder) has a completed Phase 3 trial and is rated L2. Schizotypal personality disorder, agoraphobia and manic bipolar affective disorder are rated L3. Melancholia is a subtype of major depression, so it largely overlaps with the existing approved use.

## Safety Considerations

- **Disease-level cautions from the knowledge graph** (these come from a knowledge graph and are not package insert text): the graph lists contraindication links to liver failure, liver disease and cirrhosis, epilepsy, bipolar disorder, diabetes and diabetic ketoacidosis, neutropenia and agranulocytosis, orthostatic hypotension, blood coagulation disorders, stroke and cerebrovascular disorders, myocardial infarction, benign prostatic hyperplasia, and hyperlipidaemia, among others.
- **Drug Interactions**: 360 interactions were identified. Major-level interactions include bupropion, lorcaserin, diethylpropion, dolasetron, eliglustat, palonosetron and cisapride. Moderate-level examples include acetylsalicylic acid, clarithromycin, morphine and famotidine.
- **Signal in the related schizotypal literature**: a case report described transient psychosis with psychogenic polydipsia in a schizotypal patient taking fluoxetine. Other reports describe apathy and indifference with SSRIs. Both are relevant to a population with flat affect.

Please refer to the package insert for label warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction has a high model score but no clinical trials, only a narrative review and an indirect cohort study, and no established mechanism for schizoid personality disorder. It does not yet justify further development.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Fluoxetine-specific clinical data in schizoid personality disorder, such as case series or a controlled pilot study
- Detailed mechanism of action data and a mechanistic argument specific to schizoid traits
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the India regulator, which are currently missing
- A safety plan covering the interaction profile and psychosis or apathy signals in this population
- Consideration of better-supported predictions from the same candidate: social phobia (L2, Phase 3 pediatric trial), schizotypal personality disorder, and agoraphobia (L3)
