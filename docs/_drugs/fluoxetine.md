## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Fluoxetine is generally known as a selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor (SSRI). The prediction score comes from the TxGNN model (deep learning) and may reflect similarities the model learned between related diseases, not biological evidence. Mechanistically, serotonergic modulation may reduce anxiety, depressive comorbidity and impulsivity seen in personality disorders.

However, the retrieved literature concerns mainly borderline personality disorder, depression with personality-disorder comorbidity, and a general review of cluster A personality disorders (which include paranoid, schizoid and schizotypal types). No study reports fluoxetine specifically in paranoid personality disorder. The prediction is therefore plausible but unverified.

Because no cited study directly addresses paranoid personality disorder, the evidence level is capped at L5, even though the data compiled for this report had provisionally graded it L4.

Other predictions in the same set (for context):
- **Schizotypal personality disorder**: small, mostly uncontrolled fluoxetine studies (e.g., a 1991 open 12-week study of 22 patients) and one case report of transient psychosis on fluoxetine, which is a safety signal.
- **Agoraphobia**: SSRI evidence is mainly in panic disorder, which is indirect.
- **Phobic disorder**: a completed Phase 3 trial in childhood social phobia (NCT00043537) and SSRI meta-analyses, the strongest evidence among the predictions.
- **Manic bipolar affective disorder**: evidence concerns the depressive phase only and does not show benefit in mania.
- **Histrionic and schizoid personality disorders, benign paroxysmal torticollis of infancy, Ohdo syndrome**: no relevant fluoxetine evidence.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications:** The knowledge graph records contraindication relations (not label text) with conditions including bipolar disorder, epilepsy, liver failure and cirrhosis, diabetes and diabetic ketoacidosis, neutropenia and agranulocytosis, benign prostatic hyperplasia, orthostatic hypotension, blood coagulation disease, hyperlipidemia, stroke and cerebrovascular disorders, and myocardial infarction.
- **Drug Interactions:** 360 interactions are recorded. Major-level examples include bupropion, lorcaserin, diethylpropion, dolasetron, eliglustat, palonosetron and cisapride. Moderate-level examples include aspirin, morphine, clarithromycin, famotidine and loperamide.
- **Signals from the retrieved literature:** A case report describes transient psychosis with psychogenic polydipsia in a schizotypal patient taking fluoxetine. Another reports apathy and indifference on SSRIs. Pediatric use carries a suicidality warning for antidepressants.

Please refer to the package insert for label warnings, which were not available in the data compiled for this report.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction for paranoid personality disorder rests on a high model score and indirect literature about other personality disorders. No trial or publication studies fluoxetine in this disease, so the evidence is L5. The safety profile also carries a substantial interaction burden and several knowledge-graph contraindications.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Fluoxetine-specific clinical data in paranoid personality disorder, or at least in cluster A personality disorders
- Mechanism of action data (currently missing)
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the Indian licence holders
- A safety review covering psychosis-related signals and drug interactions
- Consideration of the better-supported prediction, phobic disorder (Phase 3 NCT00043537 and SSRI meta-analyses), as a priority candidate, after verifying fluoxetine's role in that trial
