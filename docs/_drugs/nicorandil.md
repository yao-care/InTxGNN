## One-Sentence Summary

Nicorandil is a potassium-channel opener with a nitrate-like component, used to treat chronic stable angina pectoris in countries where it is approved.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH)**, but this rests on **0 registered clinical trials** and **3 publications** (one preclinical animal study and two Japanese-language reviews).
The prediction is best treated as a research question rather than an actionable repurposing candidate.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the compiled data. Based on known information, nicorandil is a K-ATP channel opener with a nitrate-like nitric oxide (NO) donor component, which produces vasodilation. The pharmacology data list its targets as the Kir6.1 and Kir6.2 channel subunits.

The proposed link to BPH runs through blood flow. The literature suggests that reduced prostatic blood flow (ischaemia) may contribute to the development of BPH and lower urinary tract symptoms (LUTS). Clinical data show an association between BPH and atherosclerotic disease such as hypertension. A vasodilator that improves microcirculation could therefore plausibly relieve these problems.

This remains a hypothesis. The supporting data come from an animal model (ventral prostatic hyperplasia in spontaneously hypertensive rats) and narrative reviews. No registered trials were found, and the titles and abstracts do not confirm direct clinical efficacy of nicorandil in BPH patients.

## Safety Considerations

Please refer to the package insert for safety information.

The interaction data compiled for this report list only pharmacological targets (Kir6.1 and Kir6.2 potassium channel subunits), not drug-drug interactions with specific agents. Because nicorandil has a vasodilating, nitrate-like action, the package insert should be checked for interactions with other vasodilators and blood-pressure-lowering drugs before any use in a new population such as older men with BPH.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The BPH prediction has a high model score but only preclinical and review-level support, with no registered clinical trials. The mechanistic link (improved prostatic blood flow) is plausible but unproven in humans, so this is a research question and not yet a candidate for development.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the Indian regulator, which are currently missing and block safety screening
- Detailed mechanism of action data (for example, from DrugBank)
- Human data on prostatic blood flow or LUTS outcomes with nicorandil, such as a small exploratory trial
- A safety assessment for men with BPH, especially regarding hypotension and use alongside alpha-blockers or PDE5 inhibitors
- Review of the full text of the three publications to confirm what they show about nicorandil specifically
