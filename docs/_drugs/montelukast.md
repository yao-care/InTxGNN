# Montelukast: From Allergic Rhinitis and Asthma to Bronchitis (Bronchiolitis)

## One-Sentence Summary

Montelukast is a leukotriene receptor antagonist originally used for allergic rhinitis, vasomotor rhinitis, and allergic and intrinsic asthma.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **bronchitis**, a label that in the supporting trials mostly maps to bronchiolitis and bronchiolitis obliterans syndrome. There are **17 clinical trials** and **20 publications** on this prediction, but the results are mixed and no clear efficacy has been established.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, montelukast is a selective cysteinyl leukotriene receptor 1 (CysLT1) antagonist. Cysteinyl leukotrienes drive bronchoconstriction, mucus secretion, airway oedema and eosinophil recruitment. These processes also occur in virus-induced bronchiolitis and post-bronchiolitis wheeze, which gives the prediction a plausible mechanistic basis.

In transplant-related bronchiolitis obliterans, leukotriene pathways are thought to contribute to fibro-obliterative airway remodelling. This is supported by rat model data (PMID 28545478).

The "bronchitis" label should be read with caution. Most trials found for it concern bronchiolitis (an infant viral illness) and bronchiolitis obliterans syndrome (a transplant complication), which are distinct entities. A few concern non-asthmatic eosinophilic bronchitis and recurrent obstructive bronchitis. The evidence is heterogeneous and mixed in outcome, and the systematic review of post-bronchiolitis wheeze (PMID 24118637) does not establish clear efficacy.

## Safety Considerations

- **Neuropsychiatric events**: Systematic review and cohort data (PMIDs 37758273, 36948487, 39836401) examine neuropsychiatric adverse events with montelukast. The US FDA added a boxed warning on these risks in 2020. Risk communication and monitoring are needed, particularly in children.
- **Disease associations in the knowledge graph** (not label text): anxiety disorder, neurotic disorder, dysthymic disorder, anxiety, hypereosinophilic syndrome and cutaneous vasculitis.
- **Drug interactions**: 470 interactions were recorded. The moderate-level ones include dexamethasone, metronidazole, miconazole, nateglinide, nitisinone and troglitazone. Further entries such as pantoprazole, omeprazole, metformin, glimepiride and morphine have an unknown severity level.

Please refer to the package insert for full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold (research question only)**

**Rationale:**
Several randomised trials in infant bronchiolitis exist, including a large Phase 3 trial, but the available systematic reviews do not show clear benefit. The "bronchitis" label also merges distinct conditions (viral bronchiolitis, transplant-related bronchiolitis obliterans and eosinophilic bronchitis), and the safety profile includes neuropsychiatric concerns, particularly in children.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- A precise definition of the target condition, with separate assessment of viral bronchiolitis, bronchiolitis obliterans syndrome and eosinophilic bronchitis
- Review of the results of the large Phase 3 RSV bronchiolitis trial (NCT00076973) and the Cochrane review (PMID 25773054)
- Mechanism of action data and the India package insert warnings and contraindications
- A neuropsychiatric safety monitoring plan, especially for paediatric use

## Other Predicted Indications (Brief Note)

- **Atopic eczema** (score 99.81%, L1): Three Phase 4 trials exist, but the Cochrane review of leukotriene receptor antagonists for eczema (PMID 30343498) did not find convincing benefit. The signal is inconsistent.
- **Asthma** (score 99.54%, L1, Proceed with Guardrails): This is effectively an on-label indication rather than true repurposing. Large Phase 3 trials exist, with neuropsychiatric monitoring as a guardrail.
- **Obstructive lung disease** (score 99.34%, L1): The supporting trials are essentially the same asthma trials. No data demonstrate benefit in COPD or other non-asthmatic obstructive disease.
- **Asthma-related traits, susceptibility to** (score 99.22%, L5, Hold): This is a genetic trait category with no clinical trials or literature, so it provides no actionable evidence.
