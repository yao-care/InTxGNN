## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the compiled data. Based on published knowledge, agomelatine acts as an agonist at melatonin MT1 and MT2 receptors and as an antagonist at the serotonin 5-HT2C receptor. This is thought to resynchronise disrupted circadian rhythms and raise dopamine and noradrenaline release in the prefrontal cortex. The pharmacology data compiled for this report also list activity at 5-HT2A, 5-HT2B, 5-HT2C, MT1 and MT2 receptors.

**Top-ranked prediction (benign paroxysmal torticollis of infancy).** This is a paediatric condition often linked to CACNA1A (a calcium channel gene). No mechanistic link to melatonergic or serotonergic signalling is evident. The very high score appears to be a graph-based prediction only, and it should not be treated as evidence of efficacy.

**More plausible candidates.**
- **Agoraphobia:** There is an indirect link through agomelatine's anxiolytic activity, since anxiety disorder is among its original indications. No trials or literature were retrieved for agoraphobia specifically.
- **Melancholia, neurotic depression, dysthymic disorder:** These are subtypes or older terms within the depressive spectrum. They largely overlap with the existing indication (major depressive disorder) rather than representing true repurposing. Supporting literature is class-level for antidepressants, not specific to these subtypes.
- **Neurotic disorder:** It overlaps with the anxiety and depression spectrum, but evidence is limited to one general narrative article.

**Other predictions (rank 6, 8, 9, 10).** Ohdo syndrome and variants, ligneous conjunctivitis, Ohdo-type blepharophimosis–intellectual disability syndrome and Keppen-Lubinsky syndrome are rare genetic disorders. They have no plausible pharmacological connection to agomelatine and no supporting evidence.
