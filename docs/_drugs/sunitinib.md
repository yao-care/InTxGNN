## Sunitinib: From Renal Cell Carcinoma to Liposarcoma
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Sunitinib is an oral multitargeted tyrosine kinase inhibitor, originally used to treat renal cell carcinoma and gastrointestinal stromal tumour (GIST).
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **liposarcoma**,
with **2 clinical trials** (both Phase 2, completed) and **9 publications** compiled for this direction. Most of this evidence covers soft tissue sarcomas in general, and only a small part is specific to liposarcoma.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, sunitinib is a multitargeted tyrosine kinase inhibitor that acts on VEGFR, PDGFR and KIT. Its efficacy in renal cell carcinoma and GIST is established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to sarcomas through anti-angiogenic and PDGFR-blocking effects.

The link to liposarcoma is indirect, however. Liposarcoma is a heterogeneous disease (well-differentiated/dedifferentiated, myxoid and pleomorphic subtypes). Its drivers include MDM2/CDK4 amplification and the FUS-DDIT3 fusion, which are not the main targets of sunitinib. The high TxGNN score therefore reflects knowledge-graph proximity and general soft tissue sarcoma activity rather than a direct mechanism.

Other predicted indications show a stronger mechanistic rationale. Dermatofibrosarcoma protuberans (DFSP) is driven by COL1A1-PDGFB fusion, which activates PDGFRB, a direct sunitinib target. Non-clear-cell and unclassified renal cell carcinoma are extensions of an already approved area. These are beyond the scope of this report but may be worth separate evaluation.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications / cautions (from drug-disease links in the knowledge graph, not label text)**:
  - Cardiac: long QT syndrome (including familial), torsades de pointes, heart failure
  - Liver: liver failure, liver disease, drug-induced liver injury, hyperbilirubinaemia (including Gilbert syndrome)
  - Endocrine: hypothyroidism, hyperthyroidism, adrenocortical insufficiency
  - Blood and other: thrombocytopenia, anaemia, epilepsy, posterior leukoencephalopathy syndrome, pancreatitis
- **Drug Interactions**: 405 interactions were identified. Of the 20 listed, one is Major (**dolasetron**, an anti-emetic, likely because of QT prolongation) and the rest are Moderate. Moderate examples include clarithromycin, dexamethasone, famotidine, loperamide, glimepiride and several insulins. Other Moderate entries are laxatives and bowel-preparation agents such as bisacodyl and polyethylene glycol.
- The drug's known cardiotoxicity (such as a decline in LVEF and hypertension) should also be considered in any sarcoma setting.

Package insert warnings and contraindications for the India products were not available, so please refer to the package insert for the full safety profile.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The two completed Phase 2 trials are single-arm and enrolled mixed sarcoma populations, so liposarcoma-specific efficacy cannot be separated out. There is no randomized evidence, and the mechanistic link is indirect. The India package insert safety data are also missing, which blocks safety screening. The evidence supports it as a research question rather than a decision to proceed.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Liposarcoma-specific results (subgroup outcomes) from NCT00474994, NCT00400569 and the published Phase 2 study (PMID 21154746)
- Mechanism of action data (MOA) and a stronger rationale for liposarcoma subtypes (MDM2/CDK4, FUS-DDIT3)
- India package insert warnings and contraindications, to complete safety screening
- Comparison against established sarcoma options such as trabectedin
- A cardiac and hepatic safety monitoring plan, given the QT, heart failure and liver-injury signals
