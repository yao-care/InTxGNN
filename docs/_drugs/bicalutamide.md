## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Bicalutamide is, however, known as a nonsteroidal androgen receptor (AR) antagonist. Androgen signalling drives hair follicle activity, so blocking the AR could plausibly reduce androgen-dependent hair growth.

The one publication in the data is a commentary on a retrospective review of 35 patients. That review, as its title indicates, reported bicalutamide improving minoxidil-induced hypertrichosis in female pattern hair loss. This suggests some clinical experience exists, but the review itself is not part of the data compiled here. The commentary is not primary evidence.

The mechanistic argument is therefore reasonable but unproven. It rests on a small retrospective observation in one specific setting, minoxidil-induced hair growth. It does not show benefit across hypertrichosis in general.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The only human evidence is a commentary letter on a small retrospective review. There are no registered clinical trials. The score of 0.9969 is a model output and is not evidence of efficacy. The AR-blockade rationale is plausible, but it is not yet supported by prospective data.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The primary retrospective review (35 patients) and any other clinical reports of bicalutamide in hypertrichosis, to assess effect size and the patient setting
- A prospective, controlled study in a defined hypertrichosis population
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data
- Package insert warnings and contraindications, including a safety plan for the relevant population (for example women of reproductive potential)
