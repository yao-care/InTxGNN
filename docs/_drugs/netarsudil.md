# Netarsudil: From Open-Angle Glaucoma to Glaucoma (Primary Hereditary Glaucoma)

## One-Sentence Summary

Netarsudil is a Rho kinase inhibitor eye drop, originally used to treat open-angle glaucoma and ocular hypertension.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **glaucoma** (and, as a second prediction, **primary hereditary glaucoma**).
For general glaucoma there are **28 registered clinical trials** and **19 publications**, but this is largely the drug's already-approved use. For primary hereditary glaucoma there is no direct trial or literature support.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on pharmacology information, netarsudil inhibits Rho-associated kinases (ROCK1 and ROCK2) and also the norepinephrine transporter. This is understood to lower intraocular pressure by increasing trabecular outflow, reducing episcleral venous pressure and reducing aqueous humour production.

The predicted indication "glaucoma" is a broad category that includes the drug's approved use (open-angle glaucoma). The prediction therefore mostly reflects a close extension of the labelled indication, not a new repurposing finding. The more novel directions (normal tension glaucoma, retinal ganglion cell effects, corneal endothelial protection) are still early-stage and small.

"Primary hereditary glaucoma" is plausible mechanistically for forms with outflow dysfunction. However, this is a model prediction only. There is no supporting trial or publication, and applicability to congenital or juvenile forms, including paediatric safety and efficacy, is unsupported.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: The data show pharmacological target interactions with ROCK1 and ROCK2, consistent with the drug's mechanism. No drug-drug interactions with specific agents were recorded.
- **Safety signals in the literature** (case reports): epithelial keratopathy, conjunctival fragility (including intraoperative and postoperative complications), punctal stenosis or closure, verticillata and honeycomb hypertrophy.

Please refer to the package insert for warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold (primary hereditary glaucoma); Proceed with Guardrails (glaucoma)**

**Rationale:**
- For glaucoma, multiple completed Phase 3 RCTs (n=411 to 756) support IOP lowering. This is essentially on-label use for open-angle glaucoma and is not a true repurposing signal.
- For primary hereditary glaucoma, the high TxGNN score (99.50%) is not backed by any trial or publication, so the evidence level is L5 and a hold is appropriate.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications for the Indian products (currently missing and a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Disease-specific clinical or preclinical evidence in hereditary glaucoma, including paediatric safety and efficacy
- Guardrails for glaucoma use: monitoring for epithelial keratopathy, conjunctival fragility and punctal stenosis or closure
- Results from the early-stage studies in normal tension glaucoma and retinal ganglion cell effects before treating these as established extensions
