## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Lenvatinib blocks several receptor tyrosine kinases: VEGFR1-3, FGFR1-4, PDGFRα, KIT and RET. These receptors drive tumour blood-vessel growth and growth-factor signalling, and both processes also matter in soft tissue sarcomas. Cutting off these signals could slow tumour growth in liposarcoma.

The most direct support is the LEADER study, which combined lenvatinib with eribulin. Eribulin is a microtubule inhibitor with a different, complementary mechanism, so the two drugs may add to each other's antitumour activity. Preclinical work also points to possible synergy with agents acting on CDK4, which is amplified in well-differentiated and dedifferentiated liposarcoma.

The evidence is limited. The only clinical study is single-arm and small, and there is no randomized comparison. The benefit of lenvatinib alone in liposarcoma remains untested.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The evidence level is L3. The only direct clinical evidence is one small, single-arm Phase Ib/II study of lenvatinib plus eribulin (NCT03526679; PMID 36129471), with no randomized trial. Package-insert safety data are also missing. The prediction is plausible and worth further research, but it is not yet strong enough for a development decision.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Randomized or at least larger controlled studies in liposarcoma, including the contribution of lenvatinib versus eribulin alone
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Package-insert warnings and contraindications, and a safety monitoring plan, particularly for the combination with eribulin
- Review of the Major interactions (dolasetron, cisapride) and the Moderate ones in the sarcoma treatment setting
