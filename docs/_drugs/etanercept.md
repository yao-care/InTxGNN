# Etanercept: From Psoriatic Arthritis and Rheumatoid Arthritis to Rheumatoid Vasculitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Etanercept is a TNF-alpha blocking fusion protein, originally used for inflammatory arthritis conditions such as rheumatoid arthritis, psoriatic arthritis, ankylosing spondylitis and juvenile idiopathic arthritis.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Rheumatoid Vasculitis**, but only **1 clinical trial** (not testing etanercept for this condition) and **20 publications** (mostly indirect or case reports of etanercept-associated vasculitis) are available.
Evidence is weak, and the benefit-risk balance is unresolved.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, etanercept is a soluble TNF receptor fusion protein that neutralizes TNF-alpha, and its efficacy in rheumatoid arthritis is established. Mechanistically, it may be applicable to extra-articular vascular inflammation in rheumatoid disease.

Rheumatoid vasculitis is a severe extra-articular manifestation of rheumatoid arthritis, so the link to the original indication is close. However, the literature also contains reports of etanercept-associated cutaneous vasculitis, leukocytoclastic vasculitis and nephropathy. This suggests TNF blockade can paradoxically induce vasculitis in some patients. The systematic review of biologics in rheumatoid vasculitis and the review of TNF blockade in ANCA-associated vasculitis are indirect evidence, and the benefit-risk balance remains unresolved.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: 352 interactions were recorded. Major interactions include corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, triamcinolone, dexamethasone, betamethasone, budesonide, prednisone, prednisolone), deferiprone, and radiopharmaceuticals/radioimmunotherapies (iobenguane I-131, ibritumomab tiuxetan, tositumomab). Moderate interactions include metronidazole, tinidazole, rosuvastatin and simvastatin.
- **Other considerations from the literature**: Reports of etanercept-associated vasculitis and nephropathy are directly relevant to this indication, as is the risk of infections such as herpes zoster.

Please refer to the package insert for further safety information, including warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction score is high, but no trial tests etanercept in rheumatoid vasculitis, and the literature is indirect or consists of case reports suggesting TNF blockade may itself induce vasculitis. The benefit-risk balance is unresolved.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Direct clinical evidence (controlled or well-designed observational studies) of etanercept in rheumatoid vasculitis
- Analysis of paradoxical vasculitis risk with TNF inhibitors in this population
- Mechanism of action data and package insert warnings and contraindications

- **Polyarticular juvenile rheumatoid arthritis** (L1, Proceed with Guardrails): A labeled juvenile idiopathic arthritis use supported by Phase 3 trials and a meta-analysis versus adalimumab. Guardrails: paediatric dosing, infection screening, tapering strategy.
- **Vertebral disease** (L4, Research Question): A non-specific category. Evidence is indirect and covers inflammatory subtypes already included under inflammatory spondylopathy.
- **Hypermobility of coccyx** and **Kummell disease** (L5, Hold): No trials or literature, and no credible TNF-related mechanism in the data.
