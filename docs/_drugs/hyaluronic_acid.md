# Hyaluronic Acid: From Approved Indications to Dry Eye Syndrome

## One-Sentence Summary

Hyaluronic acid is a hygroscopic, viscoelastic glycosaminoglycan marketed in India in injectable, topical, oral and serum forms. Its original indication text is not recorded in the data compiled for this report.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **dry eye syndrome**, supported by **9 clinical trials** (including a completed Phase 3 RCT and a Phase 2/3 RCT) and **20 publications**, including several meta-analyses and an RCT in BMJ.
Hyaluronic acid eye drops are already widely used for this purpose, so this is largely an established use rather than a novel repurposing.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the compiled data. Based on known information, hyaluronic acid is a naturally occurring glycosaminoglycan that binds water and acts as a lubricant, which is why it is used in joint, skin and eye products.

For dry eye, the mechanism fits well. Hyaluronic acid retains water, lubricates and stabilises the tear film. It also interacts with CD44 on corneal and conjunctival epithelium, which may promote epithelial healing and reduce ocular surface inflammation. These properties address the tear film instability and surface damage that characterise dry eye.

The literature supports this. Reviews describe hyaluronic acid as superior to several other tear supplements for increasing tear film viscosity and stability, and it is frequently used as the comparator for new dry eye treatments.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: Two moderate-level interactions were identified, both with topical agents: cetylpyridinium and benzalkonium. Both are common preservatives or antiseptics; benzalkonium in particular is a frequent preservative in eye drops, so preservative-free formulations are worth considering.
- **Knowledge-graph contraindication signal**: The TxGNN knowledge graph lists glaucoma as a contraindicated disease. This comes from graph edges, not label text, and should be verified against product labelling.

Please refer to the package insert for warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
A completed Phase 3 double-masked RCT, a completed Phase 2/3 RCT, multiple meta-analyses and an RCT in BMJ support hyaluronic acid in dry eye. Dry eye is already an established use globally, and the main gaps are in India-specific regulatory and safety information.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the Indian regulator, which are currently missing and block safety screening
- Confirmation of the approved indications for the existing Indian licences, and whether any ophthalmic (eye-drop) product is registered
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Review of the glaucoma contraindication signal and a preservative-free formulation choice given the benzalkonium interaction

**Other predicted indications (for reference):**
- **Sjögren syndrome (L2):** supportive symptomatic use for dry eye only, not disease-modifying. Proceed with guardrails if pursued.
- **Xerophthalmia (L3) and atopic dermatitis (L3):** research questions. Xerophthalmia needs disambiguation from vitamin A deficiency. The atopic dermatitis trial used a multi-ingredient cream, so the contribution of hyaluronic acid alone cannot be isolated.
- **Psoriasis (L4):** preclinical only, with hyaluronic acid mostly serving as a delivery vehicle.
- **Lacrimal gland prolapse, parapsoriasis, pityriasis lichenoides, severe nonproliferative diabetic retinopathy, exanthem:** Hold, with no meaningful supporting evidence.
