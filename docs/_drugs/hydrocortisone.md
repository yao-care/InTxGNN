## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data for hydrocortisone is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on its known drug class, hydrocortisone is a glucocorticoid with anti-inflammatory and immunosuppressive effects.

Alopecia areata is a T-cell-mediated autoimmune disease in which the immune system attacks hair follicles. Local glucocorticoid action could plausibly suppress the immune cell infiltrate around the follicle.

There is an important caveat. Hydrocortisone is a low-potency steroid. In the one randomized trial found, it served as the comparator for a higher-potency steroid (clobetasol), not as the main therapy. The evidence therefore supports a steroid class effect more than hydrocortisone-specific efficacy.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
One completed Phase 3 randomized trial in children with alopecia areata used hydrocortisone 1% cream, and older case series used intralesional hydrocortisone, giving an evidence level of L2. However, hydrocortisone was only the low-potency comparator, so hydrocortisone-specific efficacy is not established.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- A trial that tests hydrocortisone as the primary therapy, ideally versus placebo
- Comparison against higher-potency steroids and current standard treatments
- Confirmation of a topical or intralesional product route, since the listed local products are injectable or oral
- Package insert warnings and contraindications, and detailed mechanism of action data
- A safety plan covering systemic steroid exposure and the interactions listed above
