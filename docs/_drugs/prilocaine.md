# Prilocaine: From Premature Ejaculation to Neuralgia

## One-Sentence Summary

Prilocaine is an amide-type local anaesthetic, used topically in India and listed in the knowledge graph for premature ejaculation and psychologic dyspareunia.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Neuralgia** (the top-ranked prediction with real supporting evidence), with **2 clinical trials** and **14 publications** currently supporting this direction.
The evidence is mostly small, dated studies of the lidocaine/prilocaine combination (EMLA), so the prilocaine-specific signal is weak.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the compiled data. Based on known pharmacology, prilocaine is a sodium channel blocker local anaesthetic. It reduces nerve excitability and ectopic discharge in peripheral nociceptors.

The original indications (premature ejaculation and dyspareunia) are treated by topically reducing sensory input from the genital area. Neuralgia, particularly postherpetic neuralgia (PHN), is also driven by hyperexcitable peripheral sensory fibres. A topical sodium channel blocker is therefore mechanistically plausible for localised neuropathic pain.

Historical reports describe the lidocaine/prilocaine eutectic mixture (EMLA) in PHN, including pharmacokinetic data. However, the evidence is small and dated, and it uses a combination product, so the independent contribution of prilocaine is unclear.

Other predictions have much weaker support. Several (papillary conjunctivitis, manic bipolar disorder, bronchitis, migraine with brainstem aura and rosacea conjunctivitis) rest on model prediction alone with no trials or literature. Literature for atopic eczema and allergic asthma mainly signals safety risk rather than benefit.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications flagged in the knowledge graph (not label text):** hypertension, epilepsy, kidney disease, liver disease, diabetes mellitus, coronary artery disease and myocardial infarction, heart conduction disease and atrioventricular block, respiratory failure, angle-closure glaucoma, myasthenia gravis and other neuromuscular junction disease, butyrylcholinesterase deficiency, hemoglobinopathy, and hyperthyroidism.
- **Drug interactions:** 22 interactions were identified, most rated Major. Many involve agents that can cause methemoglobinemia, including:
  - Dapsone (oral and topical)
  - Primaquine, chloroquine, quinine and tafenoquine
  - Nitrites and nitric oxide (amyl nitrite, nitrous acid)
  - Rasburicase
  - Sulfasalazine
  - Silver nitrate and silver sulfadiazine
  - Other local anaesthetics (lidocaine, topical benzocaine, topical cinchocaine)
  - Metoclopramide

  Moderate (liposomal morphine) and Minor (hyaluronidase) interactions were also reported.
- **Literature safety signals:** methemoglobinemia and seizures after EMLA use in a young child with atopic dermatitis, higher systemic absorption through compromised skin, contact allergy, and purpura.

Package insert warnings were not available in the compiled data, so refer to the package insert for full safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prilocaine-specific evidence is limited to small, dated studies of the lidocaine/prilocaine combination (EMLA). It is supported by a plausible sodium-channel mechanism for neuralgia, but it does not isolate prilocaine's contribution. All other predictions rest on model scores alone or on safety-oriented literature. Neuralgia is worth pursuing as a research question rather than a go decision.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the India regulatory source (currently a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Controlled studies of topical lidocaine/prilocaine in neuralgia that include a comparator and clearly separate the effect of prilocaine
- A safety plan for methemoglobinemia risk, covering the interacting drugs, barrier-compromised skin, and young or vulnerable patients
- Confirmation of route and formulation compatibility with the intended neuropathic pain use
