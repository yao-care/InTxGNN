# Doxorubicin: From Lymphoma and Leukaemia to Ewing Sarcoma

## One-Sentence Summary

Doxorubicin is an anthracycline cytotoxic drug, originally used for acute lymphoblastic leukaemia, Hodgkin and non-Hodgkin lymphoma, mantle cell lymphoma and related blood cancers.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Ewing sarcoma**,
with **29 clinical trials** and **20 publications** currently supporting this direction, including several randomised Phase 3 trials in which doxorubicin is part of the standard regimen.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data for this drug is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, doxorubicin is a topoisomerase II inhibitor and DNA intercalator, and its efficacy in lymphoma and leukaemia is well established. Mechanistically, it may be applicable to Ewing sarcoma.

Ewing sarcoma is a fast-growing small round cell tumour of bone and soft tissue, mostly affecting children, adolescents and young adults. Cytotoxic drugs that damage DNA in rapidly dividing cells are the mainstay of treatment. Doxorubicin is already a core part of standard multi-agent regimens such as VDC/IE (vincristine, doxorubicin, cyclophosphamide alternating with ifosfamide and etoposide) and VIDE. In this case, the prediction therefore matches current practice rather than representing a purely novel idea.

Evidence for the other predicted indications is much weaker. Rhabdomyosarcoma has Phase 3 evidence only in mixed soft tissue sarcoma populations. Pleuropulmonary blastoma is supported by case series and registry data. The remaining predictions rest mainly on case reports, preclinical work or the model score alone.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindication-related conditions (from the knowledge graph, not label text)**: liver failure and liver disease, hyperbilirubinaemia, heart disease (including cardiomyopathy, heart failure, heart conduction disease and sudden cardiac arrest), neutropenia, leukopenia, agranulocytosis, thrombocytopenia, anaemia, and infectious disease.
- **Cardiotoxicity**: cumulative anthracycline cardiotoxicity requires cardiac monitoring and dose caps. Two of the Ewing sarcoma trials above used dexrazoxane for cardioprotection.
- **Secondary leukaemia**: the literature reports therapy-related acute myeloid leukaemia after topoisomerase II inhibitors, including anthracyclines.
- **Drug interactions**: 665 interactions were recorded. Major ones include dolasetron, cisapride and papaverine. Moderate ones include several antiemetics (ondansetron, granisetron, palonosetron, aprepitant, rolapitant), clarithromycin, levofloxacin and dexamethasone.

Package insert warnings and contraindications were not available. Please refer to the package insert for authoritative safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Doxorubicin is already part of the standard multi-agent chemotherapy for Ewing sarcoma, supported by several completed Phase 3 randomised trials and multiple published RCTs. The evidence is therefore strong (L1). The guardrails reflect cumulative cardiotoxicity, marked myelosuppression, and the missing package insert safety data.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications for the Indian-marketed products, which are a blocking gap for safety screening
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Confirmation of the approved indication text for the Indian licences
- A cardiac monitoring plan with cumulative dose limits, plus a plan for managing interactions with antiemetics and QT-prolonging drugs
- Route and formulation confirmation (the Indian licences are all injectable, including liposomal products)

**Other predicted indications (for reference):** pleuropulmonary blastoma and primary pulmonary lymphoma are research questions. Rhabdomyosarcoma has Phase 3 support only from mixed soft tissue sarcoma populations, which gives it a "Proceed with Guardrails" rating at the evidence level. The remaining predictions (lung fetal adenocarcinoma, CML, monocytic leukaemia, vaginal and parameningeal embryonal rhabdomyosarcoma, and ganglioneuroblastoma) should be held, given little or no direct clinical evidence.
