## Tiapride: From an Unspecified Original Indication to Migraine Disorder
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Tiapride is a substituted benzamide (a dopamine D2/D3 receptor antagonist) marketed in India as single-ingredient tablets. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **migraine disorder**, supported by **8 publications** (including 1 randomised, double-blind pilot study and several older controlled studies) but **no registered clinical trials** in the data compiled for this report.

*Note on evidence level:* The compiled data assign L2 on the basis of one randomised pilot study and several controlled studies. Strictly under the grading rules, which require a completed Phase 2/3 RCT, this is better read as a low L2 or high L3. The RCT is a small pilot study, and the controlled studies are from the 1980s.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available from the compiled sources. Based on general pharmacology, tiapride is a selective dopamine D2/D3 receptor antagonist. This rationale is inferred from general knowledge, not from the supplied data.

Dopaminergic activity is thought to contribute to the premonitory symptoms of migraine, such as nausea, yawning and irritability. Central D2 antagonism is a recognised mechanism behind the antiemetic and antimigraine effects of drugs such as metoclopramide and domperidone. Related benzamides such as sulpiride have also been used for migraine prevention.

The original indication is not recorded in the Indian licence data. Tiapride's mechanism, however, fits migraine well, and this is consistent with the high TxGNN score. The literature below also shows tiapride used for headache and migraine, mostly in older European studies.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The mechanistic fit and literature signal are encouraging, and the TxGNN score is very high. However, the human evidence is one small pilot RCT plus mostly decades-old, methodologically limited studies, with no registered trials. Safety information is also unavailable, so a move forward is premature.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The Indian package insert (warnings, contraindications and approved indications), which is required for safety screening
- Confirmed mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Full-text appraisal of the 2022 tiapride vs topiramate RCT and the controlled studies from the 1980s
- A safety review for long-term use in migraine prophylaxis, including dopamine-antagonist class effects
- Evaluation of whether a confirmatory, adequately powered randomised trial in migraine is justified
