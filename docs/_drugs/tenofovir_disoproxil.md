# Tenofovir Disoproxil: From Chronic Hepatitis B and HIV Infection to Simian Immunodeficiency Virus Infection

## One-Sentence Summary

Tenofovir disoproxil is an antiviral originally used to treat chronic hepatitis B virus infection, HIV infection and AIDS.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **simian immunodeficiency virus infection**, but this is an animal-model disease, supported by **0 registered clinical trials** and **20 publications** (mostly preclinical macaque studies and reviews).
The data therefore mainly reinforce the existing HIV indication rather than point to a new human indication.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, tenofovir disoproxil is a nucleotide reverse transcriptase inhibitor. Its efficacy in HIV and hepatitis B is established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to related retroviruses.

Simian immunodeficiency virus (SIV) and the chimeric SHIV are lentiviruses closely related to HIV-1 and use the same reverse transcriptase target. Macaque studies show partial to substantial protection against SIV/SHIV acquisition with tenofovir disoproxil fumarate (TDF), alone or combined with emtricitabine.

However, SIV infection is a preclinical or veterinary model, not a human disease. The prediction is biologically sensible, but it validates the existing HIV indication rather than creating a new one for patients.

Other predictions from the model are weaker:
- **Feline acquired immunodeficiency syndrome**: a veterinary indication with a single 2023 publication (combination antiretroviral therapy in FIV-infected cats). It has limited value for human repurposing.
- **Neurodevelopmental disorder with ataxic gait, absent speech, and decreased cortical white matter**: score 99.95%, but no trials, no literature and no plausible mechanistic link. It is likely a knowledge-graph artefact.
- **Obsolete familial combined hyperlipidemia**: no evidence, the disease term is obsolete, and tenofovir has no lipid-lowering mechanism.

## Safety Considerations

The data compiled for this report contain no package-insert warnings, contraindications or drug-interaction records. Please refer to the package insert for safety information.

The knowledge graph links the drug to a number of conditions as potential contraindications. These come from graph edges, not label text, and should be verified against the label. The main groups are:
- **Cardiac rhythm**: long QT syndrome, familial long QT syndrome, torsades de pointes
- **Liver**: liver failure, liver disease, fatty liver disease, non-alcoholic steatohepatitis, gallbladder disease, hepatitis C virus infection
- **Kidney and bone/mineral**: kidney disease, Fanconi renotubular syndrome, hypophosphatemia, osteomalacia, rickets
- **Metabolic**: lactic acidosis, hypertriglyceridemia, obesity
- **Neuropsychiatric**: epilepsy, anxiety disorder, dysthymic disorder, delusional disorder, paranoid schizophrenia

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests only on preclinical macaque studies and reviews (evidence level L4), with no clinical trials. SIV infection is an animal model, so the findings support the existing HIV indication rather than a new human one. The other predicted indications have little or no supporting evidence.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- A clear human-disease target, since SIV and feline AIDS are not human indications. Any repurposing work should look at other human conditions.
- Mechanism of action data for tenofovir disoproxil, which is not available.
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the Indian regulator, which are required before safety screening can proceed.
- Review of the knowledge-graph contraindication signals (liver, kidney, bone and QT-related) against the label.
- Remapping of the obsolete disease term (familial combined hyperlipidemia) and verification of the unsupported rare neurodevelopmental prediction before any further consideration.
