# Mitoxantrone: From Acute Myeloid Leukemia to Upper Aerodigestive Tract Neoplasm

## One-Sentence Summary

Mitoxantrone is a topoisomerase II-inhibiting anthracenedione chemotherapy, originally used for acute myeloid leukemia.
The TxGNN model's top-ranked prediction is **upper aerodigestive tract neoplasm**, but it has **no registered clinical trials** and only indirect literature support.
A closely related prediction, **head and neck cancer** (rank 7), has the strongest evidence of the eight predictions: **6 mapped trials** and a published 2025 phase Ib study of liposomal mitoxantrone.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on the literature, mitoxantrone is an anthraquinone antineoplastic agent with a mechanism similar to the anthracyclines. It acts as a topoisomerase II inhibitor and DNA intercalator, and it is used in acute leukemias.

Upper aerodigestive tract tumors (head, neck, nasopharynx, salivary gland) are chemosensitive solid tumors, so a cytotoxic DNA-damaging agent is mechanistically plausible. Older phase II studies in head and neck cancer (including nasopharyngeal carcinoma, adenoid cystic carcinoma, and salivary gland malignancy with cisplatin) reported limited single-agent activity. A high TxGNN score alone does not substitute for clinical evidence, and no registered trials are mapped to this broad category. Its evidence overlaps with the head and neck cancer entry below.

## Safety Considerations

- **Key Warnings and Contraindications**: Label-based data are not available. The knowledge graph links mitoxantrone to the following conditions as contraindications: liver failure, liver disease, gallbladder disease, heart failure, epilepsy, thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, agranulocytosis, leukopenia, anemia, macrocytic anemia, infectious disease, fungal infectious disease and dermatophytosis. These are not taken from label text and must be confirmed against the package insert.
- **Drug Interactions**: 355 interactions were identified. Major: dolasetron. Moderate: clarithromycin, eliglustat, rolapitant. Minor: levofloxacin. Many others are of unknown severity (for example metformin, lansoprazole, amphotericin B, morphine, omeprazole).

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The top-ranked prediction has no registered trials and only older, mostly negative or modest phase II literature, so evidence stays at L4 despite a 99.78% model score. The related head and neck cancer entry is rated L1 in the data, but that rests on a phase III trial that is not yet recruiting and has no results. L2 is the more defensible level, and the liposomal formulation is not the same product as the conventional injections registered in India.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications for the Indian products (currently a blocking gap)
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Results from the liposomal mitoxantrone nasopharyngeal and head and neck trials, particularly the phase III NCT05717764
- Defined sub-indications (for example nasopharyngeal carcinoma) in place of the broad "upper aerodigestive tract neoplasm" category, with a review of overlap with the head and neck cancer entry
- A decision on whether conventional mitoxantrone injection or a liposomal formulation is the relevant product
- A hematologic and cardiac safety monitoring plan
