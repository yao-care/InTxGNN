# Moclobemide: From Anxiety Disorder to Agoraphobia

## One-Sentence Summary

Moclobemide is a reversible inhibitor of monoamine oxidase A, originally used for anxiety disorder, neurotic disorder and dysthymic disorder.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **agoraphobia**, with **no registered clinical trials** but **10 publications** (2 randomised controlled trials among them) currently supporting this direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, moclobemide is a reversible inhibitor of monoamine oxidase A (RIMA). It raises synaptic serotonin, norepinephrine and dopamine. This is an inference from the drug class and not from the supplied record. The pharmacology data compiled for this report confirm that it targets MAO-A and MAO-B.

Agoraphobia usually occurs together with panic disorder, and it sits within the same family of anxiety disorders as moclobemide's original indications. Panic disorder with agoraphobia responds to serotonergic and noradrenergic modulation, including SSRIs, tricyclic antidepressants and classical MAO inhibitors. This makes a benefit from moclobemide mechanistically plausible.

The published studies reinforce this. They include randomised trials in panic disorder with agoraphobia and open-label work in social phobia, a closely related condition.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: The pharmacology data show that moclobemide acts on monoamine oxidase A (MAOA) and monoamine oxidase B (MAOB). No specific interacting drugs or severity levels were identified. As with other MAO inhibitors, combinations with serotonergic or sympathomimetic agents need caution.

Please refer to the package insert for warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Two randomised controlled trials in panic disorder with agoraphobia, plus several reviews, support efficacy, and the drug is already marketed in India. However, no clinical trial is registered for this indication, and the key safety and mechanism data are missing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the manufacturers (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Confirmation of the approved indication text for the Indian registrations
- Review of the two RCTs for agoraphobia-specific outcomes, since both enrolled panic disorder patients with or without agoraphobia
- A safety monitoring plan, with attention to interactions with serotonergic drugs
