# Oxyphenbutazone: From Rheumatic Disorders (Historical NSAID Use) to Rheumatoid Arthritis

## One-Sentence Summary

Oxyphenbutazone is a pyrazolone non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID) and a metabolite of phenylbutazone, marketed in India as 100 mg tablets.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **rheumatoid arthritis**, supported by **no registered clinical trials** and **20 publications**, mostly historical (1960s-1980s) clinical reports and reviews.
This is largely a rediscovery of a historical use rather than a novel repurposing, and the drug's blood toxicity is the main barrier.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on class knowledge, oxyphenbutazone is a pyrazolone NSAID that inhibits cyclooxygenase (COX) and so reduces prostaglandin synthesis, inflammation and pain. This is mechanistically consistent with symptomatic treatment of rheumatoid arthritis.

The link between the drug's anti-inflammatory use and rheumatoid arthritis is direct. Oxyphenbutazone and phenylbutazone were used for rheumatic disease for decades, and the literature includes placebo-controlled and comparator trials in rheumatoid arthritis. A laboratory study also found that a related compound, 4-hydroxy-oxyphenbutazone, strongly inhibits cytokine production, and it was reported to be in phase II trials as an immunosuppressant in rheumatoid arthritis.

The predicted indications also include two lower-ranked results. **Gout** (score 99.05%, evidence level L3) is supported only by historical reports and reviews. **Colobomatous microphthalmia-rhizomelic dysplasia syndrome** (score 99.07%, evidence level L5) has no plausible mechanistic link and no supporting studies, so it is most likely a computational artifact.

## Safety Considerations

- **Hematologic toxicity**: The literature includes reports of aplastic anemia associated with oxyphenbutazone and adverse reactions with long-term use of phenylbutazone and oxyphenbutazone. The drug has been withdrawn in many markets for this reason (agranulocytosis and aplastic anemia).
- **Other reported reactions**: Drug-induced sialadenitis (parotitis and submaxillitis) with skin and liver involvement has been described with phenylbutazone and oxyphenbutazone.
- **Drug interactions**: Pyrazole compounds have been reported to potentiate anticoagulants (1964 report). No interaction records were found in the interaction database searched.

Please also refer to the package insert for full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The evidence is historical and consists mostly of old trials, reviews and case reports. No registered clinical trials exist, and safer, better-characterized NSAIDs and disease-modifying therapies are available for rheumatoid arthritis. The serious hematologic toxicity makes renewed use hard to justify without new data.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the Indian licences (currently missing, and blocking safety screening)
- Official approved-indication text for the three Indian registrations
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- A modern benefit-risk assessment against current standard-of-care therapies, with a focus on hematologic safety
- Review of the 20 publications for relevance, since many are title-only or about other drugs
