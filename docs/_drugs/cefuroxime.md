# Cefuroxime: From Infectious Otitis Media to Urinary Tract Infection

## One-Sentence Summary

Cefuroxime is a second-generation cephalosporin antibiotic, originally used for bacterial infections such as infectious otitis media, pneumonia, meningitis, and gonorrhea.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **urinary tract infection**, with **5 clinical trials** and **20 publications** currently supporting this direction.
Most of this evidence is observational or review-level, and the use is close to existing clinical practice rather than true repurposing.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Cefuroxime belongs to the beta-lactam cephalosporin class, which inhibits bacterial cell-wall synthesis. Its efficacy in respiratory, ear, and other bacterial infections is established, and its spectrum covers common uropathogens such as *E. coli* and *Klebsiella*.

The original indications (otitis media, pneumonia, meningitis, gonorrhea, and others) and urinary tract infection are all bacterial infections. The same mechanism, killing susceptible bacteria through cell-wall disruption, applies to all of them. Cefuroxime is already used clinically for UTI and pyelonephritis, so this prediction is better described as an adjacent or near-label use than as a true repurposing.

Resistance is the main limit. Rising resistance to second-generation cephalosporins and ESBL-producing organisms is documented in the literature, and stewardship concerns apply.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications / disease associations (from the knowledge graph, not label text)**: kidney disease, pyoureter, liver disease, gallbladder disease, blood coagulation disease, *Clostridium difficile* colitis, perinatal necrotizing enterocolitis, congenital prothrombin deficiency.
- **Drug Interactions**: 143 interactions were identified. The 20 listed in the data are all of Moderate severity. Main groups:
  - Acid-suppressing drugs: famotidine, ranitidine, cimetidine, nizatidine, and the proton-pump inhibitors (omeprazole, esomeprazole, lansoprazole, dexlansoprazole, pantoprazole, rabeprazole)
  - Antacids: aluminum hydroxide, calcium carbonate, magaldrate, and magnesium salts
  - Aminoglycosides: kanamycin, neomycin
  - Balsalazide

For warnings and label-based contraindications, please refer to the package insert.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Cefuroxime has strong mechanistic plausibility and existing clinical use for UTI and pyelonephritis, supported by trials and reviews. However, the evidence is mostly observational or review-level, with no completed Phase 3 RCT in the data provided, and resistance and stewardship concerns apply.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications, so that safety screening can be completed
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Approved indication text for the Indian registrations, to confirm whether UTI is already covered
- Local susceptibility and resistance data for common uropathogens in India
- Prospective comparative data (ideally a Phase 3 RCT) against standard UTI therapy
- Renal-function dose-adjustment guidance, given the kidney disease association
