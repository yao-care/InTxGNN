# Sacubitril: From Heart Failure (Original Indication Not Listed in Registration Data) to Diabetic Nephropathy

## One-Sentence Summary

Sacubitril is a neprilysin inhibitor marketed in India, mainly as a combination with valsartan, and is used in cardiovascular disease. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **diabetic nephropathy**, supported by **1 Phase 4 clinical trial** and **15 publications**, most of them preclinical studies or reviews.

Note: the top-ranked TxGNN prediction is a rare COL4A1-related small vessel disease. It has no relevant supporting evidence, so this report focuses on diabetic nephropathy, the best-supported prediction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on the literature, sacubitril is a prodrug whose active metabolite inhibits neprilysin, an enzyme that breaks down natriuretic peptides (ANP/BNP). It is usually given with valsartan, an angiotensin receptor blocker.

Diabetic nephropathy is driven by glomerular hypertension, oxidative stress, inflammation and fibrosis. Raising natriuretic peptides while blocking the renin-angiotensin system could plausibly act on these pathways. Several rodent studies (db/db mice, Zucker obese rats, diabetic rats) report less glomerular and tubular injury with sacubitril/valsartan. These effects were linked to reduced NF-κB inflammation, NLRP3 inflammasome activity and oxidative stress. Compared with valsartan alone, the results were not uniform: one mouse study reported differing effects between models.

In humans, a secondary analysis of the PARADIGM-HF trial examined renal function in patients with type 2 diabetes and heart failure. This provides supportive context, but it is not direct proof of benefit in diabetic nephropathy.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: Six moderate-level interactions were identified: nitisinone, rosuvastatin, simvastatin, cobicistat, revefenacin and alpelisib.

For other safety information, please refer to the package insert. Known class effects of sacubitril/valsartan (hypotension, hyperkalaemia, changes in kidney function) would need monitoring in a diabetic nephropathy population.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold (research question)**

**Rationale:**
Diabetic nephropathy is the only prediction with meaningful support, but the evidence is L3: mostly animal studies and reviews, plus one Phase 4 trial with unknown status and no results. The other four predictions (brain small vessel disease, the familial hematuria syndrome, rheumatoid arthritis and hemoglobinopathy) are model output only, with no relevant clinical or mechanistic evidence.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Results or status confirmation for NCT06501651, and ideally an adequately powered Phase 3 RCT with renal outcomes (eGFR decline, albuminuria)
- Mechanism of action data and package insert warnings and contraindications for the Indian market products
- A safety plan covering hypotension, hyperkalaemia and renal function monitoring in diabetic patients
- Confirmation of the approved indications for the Indian registrations
