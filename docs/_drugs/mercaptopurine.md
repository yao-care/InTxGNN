# Mercaptopurine: From Acute Lymphoblastic Leukemia to Myeloid Leukemia

## One-Sentence Summary

Mercaptopurine is a purine antimetabolite originally used to treat acute lymphoblastic leukemia and acute promyelocytic leukemia.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **myeloid leukemia**,
with **15 clinical trials** and **20 publications** compiled for this direction. However, most of these trials test multi-drug regimens, so the specific contribution of mercaptopurine is not established.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the compiled data. Based on known pharmacology, mercaptopurine is a purine antimetabolite. Its thioguanine nucleotide metabolites are incorporated into DNA and RNA and inhibit purine synthesis, which is toxic to rapidly dividing leukemic blasts.

Its efficacy in lymphoblastic leukemia is well established, and its recorded original indications already include acute promyelocytic leukemia, a subtype of acute myeloid leukemia. Extending to myeloid leukemia is therefore partly a move within the leukemia family rather than a leap into a new disease area.

The support is plausible but not definitive. Several Phase 3 trials are multi-agent AML or APL regimens, and the data do not show that mercaptopurine's individual contribution was isolated. Direct support is limited to small or observational work, such as oral 6-MP/methotrexate maintenance in transplant-ineligible AML and metronomic 6-MP in elderly AML.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindication-related conditions** (from knowledge-graph drug–disease edges, not label text): kidney disease, liver disease, pancreatitis, gallbladder disease, cholestasis (including benign recurrent and familial intrahepatic forms), hyperbilirubinemia (including hereditary forms and Gilbert syndrome), thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, leukopenia, agranulocytosis, anemia (including macrocytic), pyoureter, and hepatosplenic T-cell lymphoma.
- **Drug Interactions**: 296 interactions are recorded. The main ones of Moderate severity are the aminosalicylates (mesalazine, balsalazide, olsalazine, sulfasalazine) and naltrexone. Levofloxacin is Minor. Others, such as omeprazole, prednisone and metformin, have unclassified severity. Literature also flags allopurinol and other xanthine oxidase inhibitors, which markedly change mercaptopurine metabolism.
- **Pharmacogenomics**: NUDT15 and TPMT variants are linked to severe myelosuppression, so genotype-guided dosing matters.

Please refer to the package insert for label warnings, as no label text was available.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold (research question)**

**Rationale:**
Mercaptopurine is mechanistically plausible in myeloid leukemia and already used in APL maintenance, but the Phase 3 trials mostly test multi-drug regimens, so its own effect is not isolated. Direct evidence in non-APL AML is limited to small pilot and observational studies, hence L2 rather than L1. Label safety data are also missing, which blocks safety screening.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Trial-level confirmation of mercaptopurine's role and outcomes in the listed AML/APL regimens
- Comparative data for 6-MP/methotrexate maintenance in non-APL AML
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the Indian regulator or manufacturers
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- A safety plan covering TPMT/NUDT15 genotyping, blood-count and liver monitoring, and interaction review

**Note on other predictions:** Acute lymphoblastic leukemia and precursor lymphoblastic lymphoma/leukemia are on-label uses (L1, Proceed with Guardrails) rather than true repurposing. Hodgkin lymphoma (indirect, L3), small cell lung carcinoma (1985 Phase 2 study and preclinical work only) and the remaining predictions rest on little or no direct evidence and are on Hold.
