## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Mercaptopurine is a purine antimetabolite. Its thioguanine nucleotide metabolites are incorporated into DNA and RNA and inhibit de novo purine synthesis. This is toxic to rapidly dividing cells such as leukemic blasts. Detailed mechanism-of-action data are not otherwise available in the compiled data, so this description rests on the general pharmacology of the drug class.

Myeloid leukemia is a blood cancer that, like lymphoid leukemia, depends on rapid cell division, so an agent that disrupts purine synthesis is mechanistically plausible. Published reports support this direction. A cohort study gave oral 6-mercaptopurine with methotrexate as maintenance therapy to acute myeloid leukemia patients who could not have a transplant (PMID 26425037). A prospective pilot study tested low-dose metronomic oral 6-mercaptopurine in elderly acute myeloid leukemia (PMID 27275453).

The evidence is capped at L3. No randomized trial isolates mercaptopurine as the variable. The Phase 3 trials listed are multi-drug regimens, so mercaptopurine's own contribution cannot be separated out.

## Safety Considerations

- **Conditions of concern**: The compiled data list kidney disease, liver disease, pancreatitis, gallbladder disease, cholestasis, hyperbilirubinemia (including Gilbert syndrome), thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, leukopenia, agranulocytosis, anaemia and hepatosplenic T-cell lymphoma. These come from drug–disease relation data, not from label text, and should be checked against the package insert.
- **Drug Interactions**: 296 interacting drugs are recorded. Moderate-level interactions include mesalazine, balsalazide, olsalazine, sulfasalazine and naltrexone. Levofloxacin is rated minor.
- Pharmacogenetic variation in thiopurine-metabolising enzymes (TPMT, NUDT15) affects the risk of severe bone marrow suppression (PMID 34442422).

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Cohort and pilot data support oral mercaptopurine maintenance in acute myeloid leukemia, and several Phase 3 trials cover leukemia regimens. However, no randomized trial tests mercaptopurine itself in myeloid leukemia, so the evidence is level L3 and should be treated as a research question.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- A trial or analysis that isolates mercaptopurine's contribution in myeloid leukemia
- Package insert warnings and contraindications for the Indian-marketed products
- A safety monitoring plan covering blood counts and TPMT/NUDT15 genotyping
